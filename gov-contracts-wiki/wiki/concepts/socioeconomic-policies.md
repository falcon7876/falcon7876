# Socioeconomic Policies

**One-line:** Public-policy overlays on the acquisition system: small
business set-asides and their four preference programs (no order of
precedence), the SBA's exclusive lanes (size, COC, NAICS), plus the
blind-vendor priority and domestic-sourcing regimes.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 14 (whole
chapter).*

## Small business fundamentals
- Statutory goals (15 U.S.C. § 644(g)): ≥23% of prime contract dollars
  to small business; 5% SDB; 5% WOSB; 5% SDVOSB; 3% HUBZone.
- Small = independently owned/operated, not dominant in its field,
  meets the SBA **size standard** for the **NAICS code** the CO assigns
  (one code per acquisition, receipts- or employee-based).
- **Jurisdiction map (who hears what):**
  - NAICS code choice → SBA OHA only, within **10 days of the
    solicitation**; GAO won't touch it.
  - **Size status** → SBA Area Office (protest to the CO within **5
    business days** of bid opening / awardee notice); CO and SBA
    protests are ALWAYS timely. GAO won't review size; COFC reviews
    SBA only for arbitrary/capricious.
  - CO must forward every size protest and withhold award 15 business
    days (or until SBA rules); awarding to a known "other than small"
    concern invites GAO-recommended termination (*ALATEC*), though
    countervailing circumstances can save performance (*TrustComm*).
- **Self-certification** in good faith for plain small/WOSB/VOSB
  status; SBA certification required for 8(a), HUBZone, and (since the
  FY24 NDAA) **SDVOSB**. Intentional misrepresentation voids the
  contract and risks 18 U.S.C. § 1001.
- **COC (FAR 19.6, applies worldwide)**: CO finds a small business
  nonresponsible → mandatory SBA referral; SBA-issued COC is
  **conclusive** and compels award; GAO reviews only bad faith or
  ignored vital info. No COC referral needed for technically
  unacceptable offers or declined options; referral IS required for
  pass/fail past-performance rejections (*Phil Howry*) — and using a
  reverse-auction agent to skip it gets sustained (*Latvian
  Connection*).

## Set-asides
- **Total set-aside is automatic between the MPT and SAT** (15 U.S.C.
  § 644(j)); above the SAT it's mandatory when the **Rule of Two** is
  met: reasonable expectation of two responsible small-business offers
  at fair market prices (*Adams & Assocs.*). FAR Part 19 stops at the
  border (except COC) even though SBA's own rules now claim worldwide
  reach — a live FAR/SBA divergence.
- **Partial set-asides** allowed (not construction, not simplified) if
  the work divides into distinct portions.
- **Limitations on subcontracting** (15 U.S.C. § 657s; 52.219-14):
  services/supplies — ≤50% of the amount paid may go to
  subcontractors; construction — ≤85%. Work sent to **similarly
  situated entities** (first-tier subs in the same program, own
  employees) doesn't count against the cap.
- No small business offers → **withdraw and resolicit unrestricted**;
  never just award to a large business (FAR 19.205(d)).
- **No order of precedence** among 8(a) / HUBZone / SDVOSB / WOSB (FAR
  19.104-1(e), settling the *Mission Critical Solutions* fight via the
  2010 Small Business Jobs Act) — the CO chooses among qualifying
  programs.

## The four preference programs
| Program | Certification | Sole-source cap (mfg / other) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8(a)** | SBA (9-year program) | J&A needed >$30M; compete >$8.5M/$5.5M | SBA is technically the prime (DoD signs directly by Partnership Agreement); follow-ons stay in 8(a) unless released; COC doesn't apply to sole-source 8(a); mentor-protégé JVs bid as small |
| **HUBZone** | SBA list | $8.5M / $5.5M | 35% of employees must live in a HUBZone; 10% price preference in full-and-open |
| **SDVOSB** | SBA (self-cert dead since 8/2024) | $8.5M / $5M | VA only: *Kingdomware* Rule of Two is MANDATORY, even for FSS orders; other agencies keep discretion (*American Relocation*) |
| **WOSB/EDWOSB** | Per 13 C.F.R. 127 | $8.5M / $5.5M | Set-asides only in SBA-designated underrepresented NAICS codes |
- 8(a) constitutional weather: *Adarand* strict scrutiny; § 2323
  preferences struck (*Rothe 2008*, later repealed); the program itself
  survived rational-basis review (*Rothe 2016*); but *Ultima Services*
  (E.D. Tenn. 2023) enjoined the **presumption** of social
  disadvantage — participants now file individual narratives.

## Competition friction points
- **Bundling** (combining requirements into a package unsuitable for
  small business): allowed only when market research shows "measurably
  substantial benefits" — ≥10% of value ≤$94M, or ≥5%/$9.4M above;
  administrative savings alone don't count. Substantial bundling
  (≥$8M DoD) needs a documented strategy; DoD posts intent to bundle
  30 days out. SBA reviews bundled SOWs.
- **Tiered/cascading set-asides**: DoD restricts them — market research
  first, written explanation when the CO can't determine small-business
  availability (FY06 NDAA § 816).
- Order-level set-asides on multiple-award IDIQs are authorized
  (FAR 19.111-2; 15 U.S.C. § 644(r)).

## Randolph-Sheppard Act (blind vendors, 20 U.S.C. §§ 107 et seq.)
- Licensed blind vendors get **priority** to operate vending facilities
  (broadly defined — cafeterias included) on federal property; in DoD
  the State Licensing Agency must hold a permit.
- Applies to **military dining facilities** (*NISH v. Cohen*, 4th
  Cir.), and takes precedence over 8(a) (*Triple P*) and HUBZone
  (*Automated Comm'n Sys.*) — but only for the "operation" of a
  cafeteria (*JW Mills*, 2022: non-operation food-service support
  isn't covered).
- Disputes go to **Department of Education arbitration**, not GAO —
  GAO dismisses SLA protests for failure to use the statutory remedy.

## Buy American Act & friends (RFO FAR Part 25)
- **Supplies**: buy domestic end products — unmanufactured = mined/
  produced in U.S.; manufactured = made in U.S. + >50% domestic
  component cost **or COTS** (component test waived). Company
  nationality is irrelevant. Not a bar — it works as a **price
  evaluation penalty**: civilian +6% (large domestic competitor) /
  +12% (small); **DoD +50%** (non-qualifying-country offers).
- Exceptions: micro-purchases, nonavailability (FAR 25.104 list),
  public interest (DoD applied it to **qualifying countries** —
  reciprocal-defense partners whose products skip the BAA entirely),
  use outside the U.S. (Balance of Payments rules instead), commissary
  resale, commercial IT, unreasonable cost.
- **Trade Agreements Act** displaces the BAA at USTR thresholds:
  award limited to domestic/designated-country/"substantially
  transformed" products — a stricter test than BAA "manufactured"
  (*Compuadd*). TAA reaches DoD only for DFARS-listed items.
- **Construction** (FAR 25.2): domestic construction *materials*,
  judged in the form delivered to the site; request BAA waivers
  **before award** (*C. Sanchez & Son* — no equitable adjustment for
  complying), though refusing a clearly justified post-award waiver
  can be an abuse of discretion (*Grimberg*).
- **Berry Amendment** (10 U.S.C. § 4862): DoD-only, near-absolute
  domestic sourcing for food, clothing, tents, natural-fiber textiles,
  hand tools, specialty metals (§ 4863), and U.S. flags — waivable only
  at Service Secretary/USD(A&S) level (the post-beret-saga fix).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- Size and NAICS fights never go to GAO — calendar the 5-day (size)
  and 10-day (NAICS) SBA clocks or lose the challenge entirely.
- The Rule of Two is discretion everywhere except the VA, where
  *Kingdomware* makes it mandatory down to FSS orders.
- A COC in hand means award — don't fight it; get the referral record
  right the first time.
- BAA is an evaluation penalty, not a prohibition; Berry IS a
  prohibition. Confusing the two is the classic DoD sourcing error.
- Watch the *Ultima* fallout in 8(a) files: eligibility now rides on
  individual disadvantage narratives, not group presumptions.
