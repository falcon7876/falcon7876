# Interagency Transactions

**One-line:** Agencies can't spend each other's money — 31 U.S.C. § 1532
generally prohibits interagency fund transfers — so every interagency
buy needs a specific statutory hook (Economy Act, Project Order Statute,
or a non-Economy Act authority), and the more specific authority always
displaces the Economy Act.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 32 (whole
chapter).*

## The framework
- **Interagency acquisition** (RFO FAR 2.101): a requesting agency
  obtains supplies/services from a servicing agency, either:
  - **Assisted acquisition** — the servicing agency awards and
    administers the contract on the requester's behalf; requires a
    signed written agreement (GT&C, documented on an **FS Form
    7600A**). RFO FAR 17.502-1(a).
  - **Direct acquisition** — the requester places its own order
    against the servicing agency's ID/IQ vehicle (FSS, GWAC, MAC);
    the servicing agency doesn't touch the order.
- **Support agreements** (DoDI 4000.19) are the broader category of
  negotiated inter-entity arrangements; the key distinction is
  whether an *acquisition* results. Both need authority — the Purpose
  Statute and augmentation rules apply either way.
- **Forms & G-Invoicing**: Treasury's G-Invoicing system is replacing
  the old paper trail — **FS 7600A** (GT&C, replaces DD 1144) +
  **FS 7600B** (the funded order, replaces the DD 448 **MIPR**).
  DD 448/448-2 are still accepted during transition (some components,
  e.g. DLA, not fully compliant until FY26) but must be placed against
  a 7600A.
- **Fiscal baseline**: without authority, transfers between agencies
  are prohibited (31 U.S.C. § 1532); receiving unreimbursed support is
  an improper **augmentation**, and reimbursements an agency isn't
  authorized to keep go to the Treasury as **miscellaneous receipts**
  (31 U.S.C. § 3302(b)). See [Funding & Fund
  Limitations](funding-and-fund-limitations.md).

## The Economy Act (31 U.S.C. §§ 1535-1536)
- The **residual** authority — usable **only when no more specific
  authority applies** (RFO FAR 17.502-2(a)(3)). Statutory conditions:
  funds available; best interests of the Government; the servicing
  agency can provide or procure the goods/services; and they can't be
  obtained as conveniently or cheaply from a commercial source
  (*Dictaphone* — GAO defers to a reasonable determination).
- May NOT be used to circumvent conditions on funds (including
  extending their availability) or to buy services the servicing
  agency is already appropriated to provide.
- **D&F required** for every Economy Act order (RFO FAR 17.502-2(b)):
  best interest + not conveniently/economically available commercially
  + one of three servicing-agency circumstances; approved by a CO of
  the requesting agency (Army: SES/flag for non-DoD orders, O6/GS-15
  within DoD per AR 5-9); if the servicing agency isn't FAR-covered,
  the requester's **Senior Procurement Executive** approves.
- **Actual cost only**: the requester pays direct costs plus indirect
  costs that are funded from current appropriations, significantly
  related, and beneficial — no profit, no fee above the actual/
  estimated cost of awarding and administering (payment above actual
  cost violates both the Economy Act and the Purpose Statute,
  B-238024). Non-working-capital DoD activities normally don't charge
  each other indirects.
- **Obligation/deobligation**: funds obligate when the servicing
  agency accepts the order; but at the end of the appropriation's
  availability, funds **must be deobligated** to the extent the
  servicer hasn't performed or awarded a contract (31 U.S.C.
  § 1535(d)) — the anti-**"parking"** rule that defeats using Economy
  Act orders to extend an appropriation's life.

## The Project Order Statute (41 U.S.C. § 6307)
- DoD-only authority to order from **DoD-owned, DoD-operated (GOGO)**
  establishments — depots, arsenals, shipyards, labs, proving grounds
  (*Kominski*). The GOGO must do the work substantially in-house:
  ≥**51% of total costs** incurred by the establishment itself.
- **Nonseverable ("entire") work only** — manufacture/overhaul of
  ships, aircraft, weapons, ammunition; construction; software when
  buying a specific end product. NOT for severable services
  (custodial, routine maintenance, training, storage) or
  level-of-effort orders.
- Fiscal advantage over the Economy Act: a project order obligates
  like a commercial contract and there is **no automatic year-end
  deobligation**. Discipline instead comes from the 90-day rule:
  at acceptance there must be evidence work will start promptly
  (usually within 90 days) and finish within the normal production
  period; if work hasn't begun by **January 1 of the following
  calendar year**, the order is cancelled and funds deobligated
  (unless the delay was unforeseeable and documented).
- Reimbursable basis only (no direct cite); fixed-price or
  cost-reimbursement; **no D&F required** (not an Economy Act order).

## Non-Economy Act authorities (use these first)
- **Fiscal rules**: obligation requires a written binding agreement,
  authorized purpose, bona fide need of the current year, execution
  before funds expire, and specific goods/services. After GAO/DoD IG
  audit findings, DoD policy imposes Economy-Act-like **deobligation
  discipline** anyway (DoD FMR vol. 11A, ch. 18): nonseverable
  services fully funded with currently available funds; severable
  services up to 12 months across fiscal years (10 U.S.C. § 3133) but
  funds not placed on contract within a year come back (*U.S. Army
  Europe*, B-323940); undelivered goods → deobligate unless lead-time/
  unforeseen delay. **Advance payments to non-DoD agencies are
  prohibited** unless specifically authorized.
- The menu:
  - **GETA** (5 U.S.C. § 4104) — interagency training on a
    reimbursable basis; servicing agency keeps the fee; federal
    personnel only (non-government attendees space-available, fees to
    miscellaneous receipts).
  - **Federal Supply Schedules** (40 U.S.C. § 501; FAR 8.4) — GSA's
    government-wide ID/IQs; orders ≤**$750K** are exempt from the
    RFO FAR 17.5 interagency procedures. See [Simplified
    Acquisitions](simplified-acquisitions.md) for ordering mechanics.
  - **AbilityOne/JWOD** (41 U.S.C. §§ 46-48c; FAR 8.7) — mandatory
    purchase from the Procurement List at Committee prices
    (list placement is reviewable — *Systems Application Techs.*).
  - **Federal Prison Industries/UNICOR** (18 U.S.C. §§ 4121-28;
    FAR 8.3) — CO written determination comparing FPI supply items to
    private-sector offerings.
  - **GWACs** (Clinger-Cohen, 40 U.S.C. §§ 11101-11704) — IT task/
    delivery-order vehicles run by OMB-designated executive agents
    (GSA, NASA, NIH); use via delegation or assisted acquisition.
  - **Franchise funds** (GMRA 1994) — fee-for-service administrative
    support (Interior Business Center, Treasury FedSource); may keep
    an operating reserve + 4% for capital — but a GAO-flagged abuse
    area, so DoD applies the deobligation policies here too.
  - **ICASS** (22 U.S.C. § 2695; DoDI 7060.06) — State Department
    consolidated administrative support at posts abroad, cost-shared
    via MOU; scrub the MOU for fiscal-law violations and manage the
    billing (a documented DoD IG problem area).
  - **10 U.S.C. § 2818** (FY25 NDAA) — reimbursable orders between
    DoD elements for facility design/construction/repair and UMMC;
    obligates like a private contract (no Economy Act deobligation),
    up to 10% contingency; still needs Recording-Statute specificity
    and a current-year bona fide need.

## DoD policy on non-DoD contracts (> SAT)
- Since the FY08 NDAA § 801, DoD may use a non-DoD agency's contract
  above the SAT **only if that agency certified** it will comply with
  defense procurement requirements (certifications posted on the DPC
  interagency-acquisition page); USD can waive in writing.
- Military-department policy adds a written **best-interest
  determination** (Army: head of requiring activity, O6/GS-15+, with
  contracting-activity concurrence): customer satisfaction, schedule,
  cost effectiveness including fees, contract administration/
  oversight, DoD-vehicle availability, socioeconomic factors; plus
  scope-of-contract, funding-propriety, and DoD-unique-terms checks
  (DRFO FARS 217.670).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- Authority selection is sequenced: specific non-Economy Act authority
  first, Economy Act only as the residual — citing the Economy Act
  when FSS/GWAC/project-order authority applies is itself an error.
- The Economy Act's year-end deobligation rule is the fiscal teeth:
  money "parked" with a servicing agency that hasn't performed or
  contracted by expiration comes back. And DoD policy now extends
  functionally similar discipline to non-Economy Act orders.
- MIPRs are dying: new interagency work rides FS 7600A/B through
  G-Invoicing, but transition is uneven — check which system the
  trading partner actually uses before the order stalls.
- Fees matter twice: Economy Act orders can't include profit above
  actual cost, and franchise-fund/assisted-acquisition fees must be
  weighed in the best-interest determination.
- For severable services through a non-DoD agency, the funds must hit
  a contract within 12 months of acceptance or they're lost — the
  servicing agency's slowness is the requester's fiscal problem.
