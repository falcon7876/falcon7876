# Contract Changes

**One-line:** How contracts get modified — formal mods vs. constructive
changes, in-scope vs. cardinal (out-of-scope) changes, and how the scope
call drives competition requirements, the duty to keep performing, and
which year's money pays.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 18 (whole chapter).*

## Vocabulary that decides cases
- **Change order**: unilateral, written, CO-signed, authorized by the
  Changes clause (RFO FAR 43.3). The contractor has no right to demand
  one.
- **Bilateral mod / supplemental agreement**: negotiated; parties can
  only agree to changes **within scope** of the original contract.
- **Administrative change**: unilateral, no effect on substantive rights
  (funding cites, POCs).
- **Constructive change**: work actually changed without Changes-clause
  process — the big litigation category.
- **REA**: contractor *request* under the Changes clause. **Claim**:
  written *demand* as a matter of right under the CDA. Different animals
  with different clocks.
- All mods go on **SF 30**; changes possible any time before **final
  payment**.

## Authority
Only the CO can change a contract (RFO FAR 43.202(a)). CORs/engineers
directing work aren't changes unless the contract says work is "as
directed" (*J.F. Allen*, 25 Cl. Ct. 312) or the CO later ratifies.
Contractor must promptly notify the CO in writing of any suspected
unauthorized change (RFO FAR 43.304).

## Changes-clause reach differs by contract type
- **Supply** (52.243-1): drawings/specs (specially manufactured items),
  shipment/packing, place of delivery. No unilateral acceleration.
- **Services** (Alt I): description of services, time (hours/days), place
  of performance — but not the overall PoP.
- **Construction** (52.243-4): specs, method/manner, GFP, **and
  directed acceleration**.
- Other unilateral levers: suspension of work (52.242-14 — costs but no
  profit), Government property (52.245-1), options (52.217-6 to -9),
  terminations (RFO FAR 49.5).

## Constructive changes — five theories (all require: authorized
official's words/conduct + not volunteered + cost/time impact)

1. **Contract (mis)interpretation.** Intent from the four corners
   first; extrinsic evidence (pre-award comms, performance conduct,
   course of dealing, trade custom) only if ambiguous. *Contra
   proferentem* runs against the Government drafter only if the
   contractor's reading is reasonable, it relied **at bid time**
   (*Fruin-Colnon*, 912 F.2d 1426), and the ambiguity is **latent** — a
   patent ("obvious, gross, glaring") ambiguity triggers a duty to ask
   before bidding (*Triax Pacific*, 130 F.3d 1469). Construction spec
   vs. drawing conflicts: specs trump drawings (52.236-21); reverse for
   supply (52.215-8).
2. **Defective specifications.** The *Spearin* doctrine (248 U.S. 132
   (1918)): Government impliedly warrants **design** specs.
   Performance specs shift design risk to the contractor; brand-name
   purchase descriptions keep risk on the Government if used correctly;
   composite specs get parsed element by element. Contractor must have
   relied reasonably, complied fully, and given timely notice; no
   recovery if it knew of the defect pre-award. Disclaimers must be
   "express and specific" (*White v. Edsall*, 296 F.3d 1081).
   Impracticability variant requires commercially senseless cost growth
   — 57%, 70%, even 200% increases have been held insufficient
   (*Raytheon*, ASBCA 50166; *HLI Lordship*, VABCA 1785).
3. **Interference / failure to cooperate.** Implied duty not to hinder:
   overzealous inspection ("nit-picking punch list," *Neal & Co.*, 36
   Fed. Cl. 600), site-access failures, unjustified disapprovals of
   submittals/subs. No recovery for **sovereign acts** (*Orlando
   Helicopter*, 51 F.3d 258).
4. **Superior knowledge.** Government must disclose vital info it knows
   the contractor lacks and can't get (elements in *Petrochem*, 837
   F.2d 1076); e.g., wrong wetlands representation = constructive change
   (*Kiewit Infrastructure W.*, 972 F.3d 1322 (Fed. Cir. 2020)).
5. **Constructive acceleration.** Excusable delay + notice/time request
   + denial or inaction + express/implied order to meet the original
   date + actual acceleration costs (*Fraser Constr.*, 384 F.3d 1354).
   Excusable delay = beyond fault/negligence of contractor AND sub;
   critical-path impact must be shown on construction claims.

## Scope determinations — the central question, two lenses
- **Protest lens (GAO/CICA)**: did the mod materially change the field
  of competition — would offerors have reasonably anticipated this
  change from the solicitation? (*AT&T v. Wiltel*, 1 F.3d 1201;
  *American Air Filter*, 57 Comp. Gen. 567). Out-of-scope mod = illegal
  sole source unless justified under FAR 6.
- **Disputes lens (CDA)**: is the modified work fairly within the
  contemplation of the parties at award? (*Air-A-Plane*, 408 F.2d
  1030). If not, it's a **cardinal change** = breach; contractor may
  refuse to perform and seek breach damages.
- Factors (both lenses): function/type of work; quantity (major
  increases = new procurements, decreases = partial T4C); number and
  **cumulative effect** of changes (200 changes in-scope, *Reliance
  Ins.*, but 1,000+ may not be, *Air-A-Plane*); time of performance.
  Broadly-conceived contracts get more latitude.
- **Performing under protest**: continuing to perform waives breach
  damages (limited to equitable adjustment, *Amertex*), and agreeing to
  a change never cures the competition violation (*Corbin Superior*,
  B-235019). Bilateral descope mods operate as accord and satisfaction
  (*Cygnus*, 63 Fed. Cl. 150).

## Duty to continue performing
- **In-scope dispute**: must proceed diligently (52.233-1(i)).
  Narrow outs: withheld progress payments, impracticality (*Spearin*),
  no clear direction.
- **Out-of-scope**: no duty to proceed — but guessing wrong about scope
  risks default. This is the contractor's hardest judgment call.

## Fiscal consequences (the part lawyers miss)
- **In-scope mod relates back** to the original bona fide need — fund
  it with the original (even expired) appropriation. Expired-fund mods
  >$4M need USD(C) approval; >$25M congressional notice + 30 days (DoD
  FMR Vol. 3, ch. 10).
- **Out-of-scope mod = new need** — current-year funds, full
  competition analysis.

## Notice clocks
- Formal changes: assert adjustment within 30 days of the change order —
  liberally construed absent Government prejudice.
- Construction constructive changes: 30 days after notice; costs
  recoverable only back 20 days pre-notice (52.243-4(d)).
- CDA outer limit: claims (both directions) within **6 years** of
  accrual (41 U.S.C. § 7103(a)(4)).
- Final payment cuts off new REAs, but not those pending or
  constructively known (*Mingus Constructors*, 812 F.2d 1387).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- Track who told you to do what: only CO direction (or later
  ratification) pays.
- Patent ambiguity = ask before bidding or lose contra proferentem.
- The scope call controls three things at once: competition, duty to
  perform, and color of money. Analyze all three before signing a mod.
