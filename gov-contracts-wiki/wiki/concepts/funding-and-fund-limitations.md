# Funding & Fund Limitations (Fiscal Law)

**One-line:** Every obligation must pass three tests — right **purpose**,
right **time**, right **amount** — enforced by the Purpose Statute, the
bona fide needs rule, and the Antideficiency Act.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 5 (whole
chapter). Deep dives live in the TJAGLCS Fiscal Law Deskbook.*

## The framework
- Constitution Art. I § 9: no money drawn from the Treasury but in
  consequence of appropriations. Default posture: spending is proper
  only when **authorized**, not merely "not prohibited" (*MacCollom*,
  426 U.S. 317).
- Authorization acts authorize; only **appropriations acts** provide
  budget authority. Major DoD pots: O&M (day-to-day, 1-year), Personnel,
  RDT&E (2-year), Procurement (3-year), MILCON (5-year).

## Purpose (31 U.S.C. § 1301)
- **Necessary expense 3-part test**: (1) logical relationship to the
  appropriation charged, (2) not prohibited by law, (3) not otherwise
  provided for. Doesn't have to be the best way — but must be more than
  merely desirable; agencies get substantial deference.
- **Election doctrine**: two equally available appropriations → agency
  picks, but the pick binds until Congress is told otherwise.
- **Expense/investment threshold** (DoD): investments (capital assets)
  take Procurement; expenses take O&M — except investment items with
  unit cost ≤ **$350K** may use O&M ($500K for contingency ops with
  SECDEF determination). Watch the **systems rule**: interdependent
  components are one aggregated "system"; no piecemealing to duck the
  threshold.
- Common personal-vs-official traps (all default to *personal expense*
  absent an exception): clothing, **food** (light-refreshment exception
  is largely dead for the executive branch per OLC despite GAO's NIH
  opinion; training/award-ceremony/conference-fee exceptions live),
  bottled water (unpotable/remote/emergency exceptions), entertainment,
  fines, licenses (but 5 U.S.C. § 5757 / 10 U.S.C. § 2015 professional
  credentials authority), business cards (allowed but agency policy
  restricts), phones in residences (31 U.S.C. § 1348; telework/data-line
  exceptions), coins/mementos (need award authority — 10 U.S.C. § 1125 /
  5 U.S.C. § 4503).

## Time (31 U.S.C. §§ 1502, 1552)
- Obligate within the period of availability; expired funds keep their
  identity 5 years for **adjustments and payments only**, then close.
- Survival exceptions: protest filed → funds live 100 days past final
  ruling (31 U.S.C. § 1558); T4D → original funds fund a timely,
  same-scope reprocurement; T4C → funds generally die, narrow
  exceptions (see [Terminations](terminations.md) fiscal section).
- **Bona fide needs rule**: obligation AND need must fall in the
  period of availability.
  - Supplies: need = when consumed. Exceptions: stock-level
    (normal inventory replacement, no year-end stockpiling) and
    lead-time (delivery/production) — neither for readily available
    commercial items.
  - Services: **severable** (recurring — grounds maintenance, dining)
    = funded by the year performed, BUT 10 U.S.C. § 3133 / 41 U.S.C.
    § 3902 allow a **12-month severable contract crossing FYs on
    current annual funds**. **Non-severable** (single outcome —
    a study, a report) = fund entirely at award, even across FYs.

## Amount — the Antideficiency Act (31 U.S.C. §§ 1341, 1342, 1517)
- Prohibits: obligating in excess of the appropriation, apportionment,
  or formal subdivision (allotment); obligating **in advance** of an
  appropriation; accepting **voluntary services**. Informal targets
  ("allowances") are not ADA-triggering.
- Purpose violations can be "corrected" without an ADA violation only
  if the right funds were available at obligation, throughout, and at
  correction (the *Bill Alexander* conditions).
- Suspected violations: investigate, initial DoD report within 2 weeks
  (DoD FMR vol. 14); administrative and criminal sanctions attach to
  individuals.
- **Augmentation** prohibited: money received from any source goes to
  the Treasury (Miscellaneous Receipts Statute, 31 U.S.C. § 3302(b))
  unless a statutory exception applies (Economy Act reimbursements,
  revolving funds, health-care recoveries, leases, conference fees
  under 10 U.S.C. § 2262...) or a GAO-sanctioned one (excess
  reprocurement costs applied to a coextensive replacement contract;
  **refunds** credited to the appropriation originally charged;
  replacement property in kind).

## E&E / Official Representation Funds
Small carve-outs (e.g., Army ORF) exempt from normal purpose rules for
official courtesies, gifts to authorized guests (caps: ~$525 non-DoD,
<$100 DoD), criminal-investigation and intelligence contingencies —
heavily regulated (DoDI 7250.13; AR 37-47), congressional notice for
uses >$500K/$1M.

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The three questions to ask of every action: whose money, what year,
  how much — purpose, time, amount.
- Severable vs. non-severable is the most common service-contract
  funding call; the 12-month cross-FY authority only works with
  **annual** funds.
- Food and coins are where careers go to be audited: find the express
  authority before approving either.
