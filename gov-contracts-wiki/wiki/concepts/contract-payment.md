# Contract Payment & Financing

**One-line:** Two different worlds with different rules: **invoice
payments** (after acceptance — Prompt Payment Act interest applies) and
**financing payments** (before acceptance — no PPA interest), plus the
machinery for EFT, assigned claims, and clawing back contract debts.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 17 (whole
chapter).*

## The core distinction (RFO FAR Part 32)
- **Invoice payments**: made after delivery/performance AND
  acceptance — final payments, partial-delivery payments, construction
  and A&E progress payments, interim payments on cost-type service
  contracts. **PPA interest applies.**
- **Financing payments**: made before acceptance — advance payments,
  cost-based progress payments, percentage-of-completion progress
  payments, performance-based payments, commercial advance/interim
  payments. **No PPA interest**; due 30 days from a proper request.
  Late financing payments defeat a default termination only if they
  actually caused the contractor's inability to perform (*Jones Oil*).
- **Financing order of preference** (32.106): private financing →
  customary contract financing → loan guarantees (dormant — no
  congressional authority requested) → unusual financing (DPAP-level
  approval) → advance payments (last resort).

## Financing methods — non-commercial
- **Partial payments**: payment for accepted discrete portions; must
  pay on request when ≥$1,000 or 50% of price (52.232-1).
- **Advance payments** (32.4): least preferred; require adequate
  security, ≤ unpaid contract price, and an agency-head public-
  interest/national-defense determination (ASA(ALT)/SecArmy level,
  non-delegable). Fit cases: nonprofits' R&D, GOCO plants, classified
  work, financially weak but essential contractors, small business.
- **Progress payments based on costs** (32.5; 52.232-16): thresholds —
  large business ≥$3.5M contracts, small business ≥SAT; need
  significant working-capital impact and a 6-month (large)/4-month
  (small) pre-delivery period. **Customary rates**: FAR 80%/85%
  (large/small); DFARS 80%/90%; class deviation 2020-O0010 runs
  90%/95%. "Unusual" (higher) rates need HCA + DPAP approval with full
  financial documentation. On IDIQs, administer per order.
- **Percentage/stage-of-completion progress payments**: DoD limits to
  construction and shipbuilding; pay only for work actually done
  (*Greenhut* — hurricane-damaged work re-reviewed); ≤80% on UCAs.
- **Provisional delivery payments** (DFARS 232.102-70) for accepted
  items under undefinitized actions: sparing, conservative, no profit.
- **Performance-based payments** (32.10): the **preferred** financing
  when practicable — objective events/measures, ≤90% of price (whole-
  contract or per-item), definitized fixed-price only, never combined
  with progress payments, never on cost-type/sealed-bid/construction.
  DoD IG has repeatedly dinged administration (undocumented "events").

## Financing methods — commercial (32.2)
Buyer financing allowed when customary in the market and in the
Government's interest, above the SAT, with **adequate security**
(liens, letters of credit, guarantees...): **commercial advance
payments** (pre-performance, capped at **15% of price**), **interim
payments**, and **delivery payments** (the only one that's an invoice
payment carrying PPA interest). Installment financing needs market
research showing it's customary.

## Prompt Payment Act (31 U.S.C. §§ 3901-07; RFO FAR 32.9)
- Trigger: PPA applies + **proper invoice** + acceptance + late
  payment. Due date = later of 30 days after the billing office gets a
  proper invoice or 30 days after acceptance. For interest math only,
  **constructive acceptance** occurs on day 7 after delivery (extend
  only with documented justification; never routine). Perishables run
  faster (meat/fish 7 days; produce/dairy 10).
- Proper-invoice checklist at 32.905(b); the Government has **7 days**
  to bounce a defective invoice (3 for meat/fish, 5 for perishables) —
  late notice shortens the payment clock day-for-day; an *erroneous*
  rejection keeps the original receipt date.
- Interest: **automatic**, no demand needed, compounded, accrues max
  one year; unavailability of funds is no excuse. Miss the interest
  too and a written demand within 40 days earns a **penalty-on-penalty**
  (100% of interest, $25-$5,000).
- **No PPA interest on disputes** over amount or compliance — those run
  through the CDA instead. PPA vs. CDA interest: PPA compounds and
  caps at a year; CDA is simple, runs from CO's receipt of a claim
  until payment. A CDA claim for unpaid PPA interest converts at
  receipt (*Technocratica*).
- Construction: 14-day clock on progress payments; contractor owes
  interest back on unearned progress payments; retainage release must
  be paid within 30 days. A&E progress payments: 30 days from approval.
- Discounts: purely voluntary; take one only within the discount
  period (improper takes accrue automatic interest). No early payment
  >7 days before due date absent agency-head determination. DoD policy
  accelerates small-business payments without moving the legal due
  date.

## EFT & mechanics
- EFT is mandatory (31 U.S.C. § 3332) with narrow exceptions (overseas
  payees, classified, contingency/deployed COs, one-off payees...).
  SAM registration supplies the EFT data and is required pre-award
  (same exception family). Payment date = **settlement date**.
- Incorrect contractor EFT data = not a proper invoice, and if funds
  leave Government control the contractor bears the loss; Government-
  caused misdirection stays the Government's problem (52.232-33/-34(e)).
- DoD invoicing runs through **WAWF/PIEE** (electronic only, DFARS
  232.70).

## Assignment of claims (31 U.S.C. § 3727; 32.8)
Contractor may assign contract payments ≥$1,000 to a **financing
institution** — one assignee, all unpaid amounts, no re-assignment;
written notice to the CO, surety, and disbursing officer. After notice,
paying the contractor does NOT discharge the debt to the assignee
(*Tuftco*). Assignees are shielded from recoupment, and a **no-setoff
commitment** (52.232-23 Alt I) protects them even from the contractor's
independent liabilities (*Bank of America*); DoD defaults to no-setoff
absent a high-level determination.

## Contract debts (32.6)
- Debt = amounts paid the contractor isn't entitled to (defective
  pricing reductions, excess financing payments, CAS adjustments,
  reinspection costs, duplicate payments, default damages...).
- Roles: **CO** determines the debt and demands payment (but never
  collects or nets it out); **payment office** collects; disbursing
  officer handles erroneous-payment cases.
- Demand letter contents are scripted (32.604): amount, accounting
  distribution, interest terms (30-day fuse for non-clause debts),
  offset warning, deferment/installment option.
- Collection ladder: voluntary payment → voluntary offset →
  withholding (after 30 days) → administrative offset (including
  Treasury Offset Program against tax refunds). Delinquent >180 days →
  mandatory transfer to Treasury. **An appeal does not suspend
  collection** (32.607-2(a)(2)), though deferment is available for
  small/financially weak contractors pending appeal.
- Compromise: <$100K at the finance office (never the CO); >$100K goes
  to DOJ. Collected funds hit the Miscellaneous Receipts Statute —
  general fund of the Treasury unless an exception applies (see
  [Funding & Fund Limitations](funding-and-fund-limitations.md)).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- Classify the payment first: financing payments carry no PPA
  interest, so mislabeling an invoice payment as financing (or vice
  versa) changes the remedy entirely.
- The 7-day defective-invoice notice is the Government's cheapest
  defense — a late bounce eats into the 30-day payment window.
- Contractors: a disputed invoice earns CDA interest only after you
  file a claim; don't sit on a payment dispute expecting PPA interest.
- COs determine debts but must never collect them — netting a debt
  against the next invoice yourself is a FAR 32.602 violation.
- Once an assignee gives notice, pay the assignee; paying the
  contractor means paying twice.
