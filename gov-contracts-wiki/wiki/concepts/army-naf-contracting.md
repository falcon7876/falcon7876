# Army Nonappropriated Fund (NAF) Contracting

**One-line:** NAFIs spend money that never touches the Treasury, so the
FAR, CICA, the ADA, and most procurement statutes don't apply — the
rulebook is AR 215-4, and the protest/disputes landscape is a doctrinal
minefield reshaped by *Slattery*.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 31 (whole
chapter).*

## What NAF/NAFIs are
- **NAF** = monies not appropriated by Congress and held apart from
  the Treasury (MWR fees, exchange profits, recycling proceeds).
  A **NAFI** is a Government **instrumentality** (per *Standard Oil
  Co. v. Johnson*, 316 U.S. 481 (1942)) that administers those funds —
  an "arm of the Government," but its obligations are payable from
  NAF, **not** appropriated funds.
- Consequence chain: no appropriated funds → **no ADA exposure** for
  pure-NAF obligations, **no CICA**, **no FAR** (FAR 1.104 scope),
  and most procurement statutes (Small Business Act, Buy American Act
  for NAF construction) simply don't apply.
- But labor standards DO apply: **Davis-Bacon, Copeland, CWHSSA,
  Service Contract Act, Walsh-Healey** all reach NAF contracts.

## The AR 215-4 rulebook
- Competition: statute doesn't require it, but AR 215-4 demands
  **maximum practicable competition** above **$10K** — solicit at
  least three sources, try to get at least two responses; document
  sole-source justifications.
- **Warrants**: contracting authority flows through NAF channels —
  the contracting office (e.g., installation NAF contracting or the
  Army NAF contracting activity) issues warrants; the senior
  contracting official (CAO) can be warranted up to **$50M**;
  **ordering officers** up to $25K; BPA callers per appointment.
  **Emergency purchases** by unwarranted personnel can be valid if
  reported within **2 working days**.
- Methods: **negotiation is the preferred method**; sealed bidding
  requires CAO approval; **CPPC is prohibited** (same as the FAR
  world). Contract length caps: **5 years** for SCA-covered service
  contracts, **10 years** general (longer with approval).
- APF/NAF mixing is tightly controlled: **UFM** (uniform funding and
  management, 10 U.S.C. § 2491) lets APF be treated as NAF for MWR
  support, and **MWRUSA**-type practices govern the exceptions —
  otherwise using the wrong color of money is an augmentation
  problem on the APF side (see [Funding & Fund
  Limitations](funding-and-fund-limitations.md)).

## Special NAF instruments
- **Concession contracts** (long-term and short-term): the
  contractor pays the NAFI (a percentage of receipts or flat fee)
  for the privilege of operating on the installation — revenue
  generators, not procurements.
- **Entertainment contracts**: short-form instruments for acts and
  performances.
- **Vending and amusement machines**: watch **Randolph-Sheppard** —
  blind-vendor priority can reach vending on federal property even
  in the NAF world (see [Socioeconomic
  Policies](socioeconomic-policies.md)).
- **Commercial sponsorship** (10 U.S.C. § 2494 note; AR 215-1):
  companies pay/provide goods for the right to be associated with
  MWR events — this is a **marketing tool, not contracting**, run
  through MWR marketing channels, though legal review still checks
  ethics/endorsement issues.
- **Interagency NAFI support** (10 U.S.C. § 2492): NAFIs and armed
  forces elements may provide/exchange goods and services — but the
  DoD IG's *Asiel* opinions read it narrowly: MWR-type purposes
  only, and the transaction must benefit the NAFI. The **Economy Act
  does NOT extend to NAFIs** (it moves appropriated funds between
  federal agencies — see [Interagency
  Transactions](interagency-transactions.md)).

## Protests — who will hear you?
- **GAO has no jurisdiction** over NAFI procurements: a NAFI is not
  a "federal agency" under CICA and its money isn't appropriated
  (*Defense Systems Vending* line). Exceptions where GAO WILL take
  it: an **appropriated-fund CO acts as a conduit** for the NAFI, or
  there is **pervasive involvement** of APF personnel/funds in the
  procurement (*Barbarosa Reiseservice*; *Thayer Gate Development*;
  *Premier Vending*).
- The real forum is an **agency protest to the NAF contracting
  officer** under AR 215-4: pre-award anytime before award;
  post-award within **10 days**; decision appealable up the NAF
  chain, and that appellate decision is **final** — no judicial
  second bite in most cases.

## Disputes — the *Slattery* landscape
- The **CDA does not apply** to NAFI contracts, with a statutory
  carve-in for the **exchanges** (AAFES, NEXCOM, MCX — 41 U.S.C.
  § 7102(a)); *Pacrim Pizza v. Pirie* enforced the line for
  non-exchange NAFIs.
- **Tucker Act**: the old "NAFI doctrine" (no COFC jurisdiction
  because no appropriated funds could pay the judgment) was
  **abrogated by *Slattery v. United States*, 635 F.3d 1298 (Fed.
  Cir. 2011) (en banc)** — jurisdiction turns on whether the NAFI's
  activity is a **governmental function**, not on the funding
  source. Post-*Slattery*, COFC contract suits against NAFIs are
  generally available; *AINS v. United States* is no longer good law.
- **ASBCA**: takes NAFI appeals where the contract contains a
  disputes clause naming it (*Parsons Evergreene* — board took
  jurisdiction; Fed. Cir. affirmed on other grounds); but a clause
  that makes the board's decision final can stick — *Minesen Co.*
  (contractor bargained away Federal Circuit review).
- The **Christian doctrine does NOT reach NAFI contracts** — clauses
  required by AR 215-4 but omitted are not read in (*F2M* line),
  because the FAR (the source of Christian incorporation) doesn't
  govern. Draft the disputes clause you want, because nothing fills
  the gap.
- **EAJA** doesn't apply to NAFI cases (again excepting the
  exchanges).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- Do not cite the FAR in a NAF dispute — AR 215-4 is the rulebook,
  and the Christian doctrine won't rescue an omitted clause. What's
  written in the contract is all there is.
- Protest counseling flips: tell a disappointed NAF offeror the GAO
  door is closed unless APF conduit/pervasive involvement exists,
  and the 10-day agency-protest window is the real deadline.
- *Slattery* changed the disputes answer — older authority saying
  "no Tucker Act jurisdiction over NAFIs" is abrogated; check the
  date of any case before relying on it.
- A *Minesen*-style finality clause is enforceable: contractors
  signing NAFI contracts can bargain away appellate review without
  realizing it.
- The Economy Act cannot move support to or from a NAFI; use 10
  U.S.C. § 2492 and confirm the MWR-purpose and benefit-to-the-NAFI
  limits from the *Asiel* opinions.
