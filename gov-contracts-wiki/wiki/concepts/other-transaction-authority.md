# Other Transaction Authority (OTA)

**One-line:** OTs are legally binding contracts defined by what they're
NOT — not procurement contracts, grants, or cooperative agreements — so
the FAR, CICA, CDA, TINA, CAS, and Bayh-Dole all fall away, and
everything (IP, changes, termination, disputes) is whatever the
Agreements Officer negotiates.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 33 (whole
chapter).*

## What an OT is
- Defined in the negative: a transaction "other than" a procurement
  contract, grant, or cooperative agreement (*Hydraulics Int'l*, 161
  Fed. Cl. 167 (2022)) — but still an enforceable contract with the
  United States.
- History: NASA got the first OTA in the Space Act of 1958; DARPA in
  1989; DoD-wide prototype authority via § 845 of the FY94 NDAA, made
  permanent by § 815 of the FY16 NDAA. Congress has expanded it in
  nearly every NDAA since and openly scolds risk-averse lawyers for
  underusing it (S. Rep. 115-125). Only DoD and DHS have prototype
  authority; most other agencies have research-only OTA.
- **Three DoD types**:
  1. **Research OT** — 10 U.S.C. § 4021 (basic/applied/advanced
     research; used when a contract/grant/cooperative agreement isn't
     feasible or appropriate; as a TIA it picks up the DoDGARs).
  2. **Prototype OT** — 10 U.S.C. § 4022 (the workhorse; this
     chapter's focus).
  3. **Production OT** — § 4022(f) follow-on production after a
     successful prototype; plus new § 4022(j) (FY26 NDAA) for
     fielding existing capabilities in exceptional circumstances.

## What law applies (and doesn't)
- **Inapplicable**: CICA, TINA, CAS, CDA, GAO bid-protest procedures,
  CPPC prohibition, Buy American Act, Bayh-Dole, 10 U.S.C. data-rights
  statutes, FAR/DFARS, DoDGARs (except TIAs), the UCC.
- **Applicable**: fiscal law in full (purpose/time/amount, ADA;
  OTs are recordable obligations under 31 U.S.C. § 1501(a)(9) —
  B-333150), Comptroller General records access (OTs >$5M), the
  Tucker Act, criminal law (Title 18), the **Procurement Integrity
  Act**, ethics rules (*Bader v. United States*, Fed. Cir. 2024 —
  § 207(c) cooling-off applied to consortium OT work), export
  controls, trade-secret statutes, FOIA/Privacy Act (with a 5-year
  FOIA withholding window for Research OT submissions, § 4021(i)).
- Nothing bars **voluntarily importing** protections from
  inapplicable statutes (Buy American terms, unilateral termination,
  § 3861 indemnification) — often prudent with production in view.
- No dispositive list exists: for each statute, ask whether it
  reaches only "procurement contracts" or sweeps more broadly.

## Prototype OT requirements (10 U.S.C. § 4022)
- **Scope**: prototype projects directly relevant to mission
  effectiveness or improving platforms/systems/components. FY23 NDAA
  codified "prototype project" broadly: proof of concept, model,
  business process, reverse engineering for obsolescence, novel
  commercial-tech application, **agile development activity**,
  demonstration of operational utility — and the list "is not meant
  to be restrictive."
- **Participation gate** — § 4022(d) requires one of:
  1. ≥1 **nontraditional defense contractor** (NDC) or nonprofit
     research institution participating to a significant extent;
  2. all significant participants small businesses or NDCs;
  3. ≥**one-third cost share** from non-federal sources; or
  4. SPE exceptional-circumstances written determination (rare).
  - **NDC** (10 U.S.C. § 3014): not performing a fully-CAS-covered
    DoD contract for the past year — which captures nearly everyone
    but the biggest primes. NDC participation can sit at sub/vendor
    tier; "significant extent" = new key technology, significant
    share of effort, or material cost/schedule/performance impact
    (no formula — don't over-constrain).
- **Cost share** (when required): cash or in-kind; third parties may
  fund it; pre-execution costs count if the AO determines in writing
  they were incurred in anticipation of award (§ 4022(d)(2)(B)).
- **Competition**: CICA doesn't apply, but § 4022(b) demands
  competitive procedures "to the maximum extent practicable" — and
  the award must survive arbitrary-and-capricious review at COFC.
  Solicit via white papers, CSOs, pitch events, consortium calls;
  posting on SAM.gov alone rarely reaches nontraditionals but helps
  prove competitive procedures.
- **Approval thresholds** (FY25 NDAA, non-delegable above $100M):
  ≤$100M service-delegable; >$100-500M **HCA** or DARPA/DIU/MDA
  directors; >$500M **SPE** or those directors, with congressional
  notice **at the time** of exercise (FY26 NDAA dropped the 30-day
  advance requirement). Every prototype project needs its own D&F.
- **Agreements Officers** are warranted separately from COs (need
  not be a warranted CO); expected to draft from business judgment,
  not copy a template.
- **Funding**: usually RDT&E for research/prototypes but genuinely
  case-by-case (the FMR's "prototype" ≠ § 4022's "prototype");
  production rides procurement or O&M. Cost-share receipts on a
  Research OT can be reused for R&D without violating miscellaneous
  receipts (§ 4021(d)). Incrementally funded/expenditure-type OTs
  need negotiated limitation-of-funds-style protections.

## Follow-on production (§ 4022(f))
- Sole-source production contract **or** OT is authorized if:
  (1) the prototype OT was competitively awarded, and (2) the
  participants **successfully completed** the prototype project.
- "Successful completion" is undefined by statute — **define it
  broadly in the prototype OT** (e.g., "substantially met technical
  goals"); document it in the approval D&F. Consortium subprojects
  qualify individually — no need to finish every subproject.
- FY23 NDAA § 842 removed the requirement that the follow-on be
  noticed in the original solicitation — legislatively reversing
  GAO's *Oracle America* (B-416061, the REAN Cloud protest) — but
  noticing it anyway remains the transparency best practice.
  FY24 NDAA § 821: follow-ons don't re-run the § 4022(d)
  participation test. FY25 NDAA § 817: follow-on may be one or more
  contracts, OTs, or a mix.
- Non-competitive follow-on production >$100M needs high-level
  approval, the critical-national-security-objectives standard, and
  congressional notification (§ 4022(a)(2)(C)).
- Alternatively, the agency may skip § 4022(f) and use a classic
  FAR Part 6 J&A for the sole-source production award (*DRS
  Sustainment Systems* — GAO denied the protest). And a successful
  prototype performer is never *entitled* to sole-source production;
  competing the production buy is often smarter.

## Terms & conditions — you get what you negotiate
- No Christian doctrine, no automatic clauses. Negotiate: scope,
  term, payment, **disputes**, confidentiality, IP, export control,
  QA, GFE, indemnification, phasing.
- **Modifications**: bilateral unless a unilateral-changes term is
  negotiated; if unilateral changes are allowed, provide an
  equitable-adjustment-style compensation mechanism.
- **Termination**: NO automatic T4C right — draft one if wanted;
  consider awardee termination rights in cost-share deals (limited
  settlement to the last completed milestone).
- DPCAP policy memos impose required "clauses" anyway (§ 889
  telecom prohibition, eBusiness tools, reporting).

## IP in OTs
- Bayh-Dole and the data-rights statutes don't apply — IP terms are
  the most important and most dangerous part of the agreement. Many
  OTs use Bayh-Dole or DFARS 7013/7014 as negotiation baselines
  (some DPCAP memos require certain DFARS clauses), but
  **cherry-picking DFARS clauses without their definitional context
  backfires**: in *The Boeing Co.*, ASBCA No. 60373 (2018), TIA
  software funded by the Government still counted as "developed
  exclusively at private expense" under -7014 because the funds ran
  through a cooperative agreement, not a "government contract."
- Always require marking procedures and an unmarked-=-unlimited
  default; avoid buying rights in data never delivered (the inchoate
  rights problem); for AI, license both directions (vendor access to
  Government training data; Government rights to test/evaluate/
  accredit models). Align with DoDI 5010.44 IP strategy: plan early,
  buy only needed rights, price them while competition exists. See
  [Intellectual Property](intellectual-property.md).

## Protests & disputes
- **GAO**: no general jurisdiction (OTs aren't procurement
  contracts — *MorphoTrust*, *MD Helicopters*), with two cracks:
  a timely **pre-award** protest that the agency is improperly using
  OTA to avoid a procurement contract (*System Architecture IT*),
  and possibly non-procurement awards that "down-select" the field
  for a later contract (*ARiA*, 2024).
- **COFC** is now the de facto OT protest forum: *Raytheon v.
  United States*, 175 Fed. Cl. 281 (2025) claimed exclusive
  jurisdiction over §§ 4021-22 awards where the instrument is
  intended to deliver a direct benefit in products or services;
  *Telesto* (2025) reads it more narrowly (agency must have
  committed to a procurement). Earlier cases turned on the
  "in connection with a procurement" hook (*Hydraulics*,
  *Kinemetrics*, *IRTC*; contrast *SpaceX* — pure prototype, no
  procurement connection, sent to district court). Unsettled —
  watch this line.
- **Disputes**: CDA and the BCAs are out. The Tucker Act reaches OT
  breach claims (*Spectre*; *CLogic*) — but absent a
  **money-mandating provision** in the OT, jurisdiction survives
  while the damages remedy may not. Draft the disputes clause
  (and consider ADR); see [Contract Disputes
  Act](contract-disputes-act.md) and [ADR](adr.md).

## Stand-alone vs. consortium
- **Stand-alone**: direct award to one entity; maximum flexibility,
  blank-page drafting, slowest to negotiate.
- **Consortium**: agency awards a long-term OT to a consortium
  (often via a nonprofit consortium management group); the
  consortium runs competitions and awards sub-OTs to members (C5,
  DOTC, AMTC, CWMD, etc.; membership barriers are low). Faster
  awards — analogized to ordering against a multiple-award ID/IQ —
  but the agency often has privity **only with the consortium**,
  and fees skim each award. See DoDIG-2021-77 for audit findings.

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- "The FAR doesn't apply" cuts both ways: no clause you didn't
  negotiate exists. Walk the FAR's protective clauses (changes,
  termination, disputes, limitation of funds) as a checklist and
  consciously decide which to recreate.
- The participation/cost-share gate is checked at award: pairing a
  traditional prime with a significant NDC sub is the standard play
  to avoid the one-third cost share.
- Define "successful completion" in the prototype OT itself —
  vagueness there is what sank the REAN Cloud follow-on.
- Don't cherry-pick DFARS IP clauses into an OT without their
  definitions (*Boeing*, ASBCA) — draft self-contained terms.
- Protest exposure hasn't vanished; it moved to COFC
  (*Raytheon*/*Telesto*), and GAO still hears "improper use of OTA"
  pre-award protests. Paper the competitive procedures accordingly.

  Department of War" and "Secretary of War" naming (post-rename);
  cited statutes are unchanged.
