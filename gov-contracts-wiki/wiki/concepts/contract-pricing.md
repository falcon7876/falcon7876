# Contract Pricing

**One-line:** Every award needs a fair-and-reasonable price
determination; TINA supplies the data (and the defective-pricing
hammer), FAR Part 31 decides which costs count, and equitable-adjustment
math governs pricing after the change orders start.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 13 (whole
chapter).*

## Vocabulary that controls the analysis
- **Price = cost + profit/fee.** "Profit" belongs to fixed-price;
  "fee" to cost-reimbursement. Misfiling a *cost* issue as a *price*
  issue breaks the legal analysis.
- **Price analysis** = evaluate the bottom-line price, no cost
  elements. **Cost analysis** = evaluate the elements and profit/fee.
- **Cost realism** (mandatory, cost-type): adjust each offeror's
  estimate to *probable cost* for evaluation. **Price realism**
  (fixed-price): optional, permitted ONLY if the solicitation says so
  (*DynCorp*, B-407762.3); prices are not adjusted.

## Price analysis (RFO FAR 15.404-1)
- Policy trio (15.402): fair and reasonable prices; obtain **no more
  data than necessary**; price each contract independently (no
  making up losses on other work).
- Techniques, roughly in order: (1) **compare competing offers** —
  adequate price competition usually suffices; (2) **historical prices**
  (validate the circumstances of the old buy); then rough yardsticks,
  published/market prices, **IGCEs** (only required by the FAR for
  A&E/construction), market research, and analysis of offeror data
  (using the non-preferred technique is fine in a complex procurement,
  *DynCorp v. U.S.*, 10 F.4th 1300).
- Data order of preference (15.403-1): competition → sources other than
  the offeror (IGCEs, catalogs, history) → data other than certified
  cost or pricing data (DOTCCPD) → certified data (last resort, HCA
  approval below the threshold). FY20 NDAA § 803: fair-and-reasonable
  may NOT rest *solely* on historical prices paid.
- Refusing a DOTCCPD request = ineligible for award (15.403-1(d)),
  absent an HCA best-interest determination.
- Document it all in the **price negotiation memorandum (PNM)** —
  the record that wins or loses later fights.

## Cost-reimbursement pricing (FAR Part 31)
- **Total cost** = allocable direct + indirect costs + cost of money −
  credits. **Allowable** = reasonable + allocable + CAS/GAAP-compliant
  + per contract terms + not limited by Subpart 31.2.
  - *Reasonable*: prudent-person-in-competitive-business; **no
    presumption of reasonableness** — once the CO challenges a cost,
    the burden shifts to the contractor (*KBR v. U.S.*, 728 F.3d 1348).
  - *Allocable*: nexus to the contract (incurred for it, benefits it
    plus other work, or necessary to the business); benefit to the
    Government is NOT required (*Boeing North American*, 298 F.3d
    1274). On CAS-covered contracts, CAS beats the FAR on allocability.
- **Expressly unallowable** favorites: bad debts, entertainment,
  alcohol, lobbying, losses on other contracts, excessive pass-through
  charges (≥70% subcontracted triggers scrutiny). Costs of breaching
  the contract are unallowable (*Tecom II*, 566 F.3d 1037 — Title VII
  judgment would breach the nondiscrimination clause, so defense costs
  disallowed; settlements judged by the "very little likelihood of
  success" test, *Bechtel*). Unlisted costs are judged by treatment of
  "similar or related" items (31.204(d)).
- **Statutory fee caps** (10 U.S.C. § 3322(b)): CPFF R&D 15%; A&E
  design 6% of estimated construction cost; other CPFF 10%.
- **CAS** (48 C.F.R. ch. 99, 19 standards): applies contract-by-
  contract; exempt: sealed bids, ≤TINA threshold, small businesses,
  commercial items, prices set by law, FFP on adequate competition
  without certified data, <$7.5M for units with no CAS-covered work.
- Advance agreements (31.110) tame special/unusual costs — written,
  bilateral, before the cost is incurred.

## TINA (Truthful Cost or Pricing Data, 10 U.S.C. §§ 3701-08)
- Disclosure statute: submit + **certify** cost or pricing data as
  accurate, complete, and current as of price agreement. Purpose:
  level the negotiating field in sole-source settings.
- **Threshold: $2.5M now; $10M for contracts entered into after
  June 30, 2026** (FY26 NDAA). Applies to negotiated prime contracts,
  flowed-down subcontracts, and mods (of sealed-bid contracts too);
  mod threshold counts |ups| + |downs| ($1.5M down + $1M up = $2.5M).
- **Prohibited** when: at/below the SAT, adequate price competition
  (DoD: two or more offers actually received), prices set by
  law/regulation, commercial products/services, or HCA waiver
  (non-delegable). Demanding certified data despite adequate
  competition can be an abuse of discretion (*United Technologies*).
- **Cost or pricing data** = verifiable **facts** prudent parties would
  expect to significantly affect negotiations: vendor quotes, unit-cost
  trends, make-or-buy decisions, management decisions with cost impact
  (even unimplemented ones, *Lockheed*; imminent discount-program
  changes, *Millipore*). Pure judgment isn't data (*Litton*) — but
  facts entangled with judgment must be disclosed.
- **Submission must be meaningful**: physically deliver or specifically
  identify to someone in the negotiation; "the books were available for
  audit" fails (*Hughes Aircraft*); disclosure to an uninvolved
  auditor/ACO fails (*Singer*; *Sylvania*). Update by sweep data to the
  cutoff; certify within 5 days of price agreement (DoD memo). Failure
  to certify doesn't dodge defective-pricing liability.

## Defective pricing
- Data inaccurate/incomplete/noncurrent as of the certificate date →
  Government gets a **price reduction incl. profit/fee** (52.215-10/
  -11; read in via the *Christian* doctrine if omitted), **compound
  daily interest** on overpayments (*Gates v. Raytheon*), and a
  **penalty equal to the overpayment** for knowing submissions.
- Government's burden: (1) it was cost or pricing data; (2) not
  meaningfully disclosed; (3) reliance — presumed from nondisclosure,
  but rebuttable (*Wynne v. United Technologies*).
- **Dead defenses** (statutory): sole-source bargaining power, "the CO
  should have known," total-price agreement, no certificate executed.
  Live defenses: pure judgment, insignificance, actual CO knowledge
  (*Alloy Surfaces*), no reliance.
- **Offsets** for understated data allowed up to (never beyond) the
  Government's claim on the same pricing action — unless the contractor
  knowingly withheld sweep data (*United Technologies v. Peters*).
- Audit rights: agency/DCAA (subpoena reaches tax returns and
  workpapers, *Newport News II/III*, but not internal audit reports,
  *Newport News I*), GAO, and IG (broadest — includes internal
  audits, *Westinghouse*). Rights expire 3 years after final payment.
  Fraudulent data escalates to FCA/False Statements/Major Fraud, T4D
  (*Joseph Morton*), suspension/debarment.

## Pricing by method & type (quick table)
| Method/type | Key pricing rule |
|---|---|
| Sealed bidding | Part 15.4 doesn't apply (mods do); watch unbalanced bids |
| Simplified | Base reasonableness on competitive quotes; no certified data ever |
| Commercial | Catalog price ≠ automatically fair; limited DOTCCPD requests |
| Sole source | Negotiated; TINA rules apply — but sole-source alone ≠ certified data |
| Letter contract/UCA | NTE at award; price reasonableness at definitization; CO can unilaterally price (HCA approval) if definitization stalls |
| OTs | TINA/FAR don't apply; DoD OT Guide still makes pricing a "key consideration" |
| IDIQ | Price at contract level; no re-determination per order if fair at award |
| T&M | Ceiling price; price analysis before any ceiling increase |

## Pricing contract adjustments (the REA math)
- **Equitable adjustment** = reasonable cost of changed work − reasonable
  cost of original work, **plus reasonable profit**; goal is to keep the
  contractor whole, never to shift the pre-change profit/loss posture
  (*Pacific Architects*; contractor who bid at a loss still gets profit
  on added work, *Stewart & Stevenson*). Based on incurred costs, not
  market price.
- **Deleted work**: measure by the "would-have" cost of performance,
  NOT the bid allocation — the Government gets the full actual savings
  and the contractor keeps its original loss (worked examples in ch. 13
  at 13-65/66).
- **Adjustments without profit**: Suspension of Work (52.242-14),
  Government Delay (52.242-17), labor-standards price adjustments
  (direct labor only — but a retroactively imposed wage determination
  is a Changes-clause matter and DOES carry profit and indirects,
  *W.G. Yates*).
- **Proof**: claimant proves liability, causation, resultant injury.
  Methods: **actual cost** (preferred; failure to segregate costs can
  kill recovery, *Togaroli*), estimates (with substantiation), jury
  verdict (needs clear injury + no better method), **total cost**
  (last resort — impracticable-to-prove + realistic bid + reasonable
  actuals + no contractor fault, *Servidone*), modified total cost.
- **Unabsorbed overhead (Eichleay)**: Government delay + extension +
  **standby** (indefinite delay, ready to resume immediately, work
  substantially suspended, *P.J. Dick*); burden then shifts to the
  Government to show replacement work was possible (*Satellite
  Electric*). Formula: allocate overhead by billings ratio → daily rate
  → × delay days. No profit on it; no double recovery.
- **Subcontractor claims**: no privity — pass-through via the prime
  under the *Severin* doctrine; the Government must plead and prove the
  prime isn't liable to the sub (releases/lien waivers do it, *MW
  Builders*).
- **Interest**: no interest on borrowings or lost-opportunity cost;
  FCCOM only if proposed up front; Prompt Payment Act interest after 30
  days on proper invoices; **CDA interest runs from receipt of a
  certified claim** even for unincurred costs (*Servidone*) — but a T4C
  settlement proposal isn't a claim until impasse (*Ellett*).
- Legal fees: contract-administration/negotiation costs allowable;
  **claim prosecution costs unallowable** (31.205-47(f)(1)); the line
  is whether the parties were still "working together" (*Marine
  Hydraulics*). EAJA can award fees against an unjustified Government.

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The TINA threshold jumps to $10M on July 1, 2026 — check award dates
  before demanding certified data.
- "We gave DCAA access to everything" is not disclosure; hand the data
  to the negotiator and say what it means.
- Segregate change-order costs from day one — the actual-cost method is
  king and boards punish unsegregated claims.
- Deleted-work pricing by bid line items is the classic Government
  giveaway; always price the "would-have" cost.
- REA-preparation legal fees are allowable only while the parties are
  negotiating, not once positions harden into a claim.
