# Responsibility, Timeliness & OCIs

**One-line:** Three cross-cutting award gates: the contractor must be
capable (responsibility), the proposal must be on time (late is late),
and the competition must be untainted (organizational conflicts of
interest).

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 12 (whole
chapter).*

## Responsibility (FAR 9.1)
- Distinct from responsiveness: responsiveness = does the bid conform
  (fixed at bid opening); responsibility = can the contractor perform
  (determinable **any time before award** — late-arriving info counts,
  *ADC Ltd.*). Affirmative determination required before every award
  (FAR 9.103).
- **Seven general standards** (FAR 9.104-1, apply even if unstated):
  financial resources; delivery/performance schedule; performance
  record; integrity/business ethics; organization & experience;
  equipment/facilities; otherwise qualified & eligible. Ability to
  *obtain* resources satisfies the resource prongs (*McLaughlin*).
- Seriously deficient recent performance = presumed nonresponsible
  (FAR 9.104-3(b)); no prior T4D needed (*Schenker Panamericana*).
  Bankruptcy alone can't drive nonresponsibility (11 U.S.C. § 525;
  *Bender Shipbuilding*) — but a real financial-risk analysis can
  (*Global Crossing*). "Otherwise eligible" picks up unpaid federal
  tax liability/felony convictions (52.209-11), accounting-system
  adequacy for cost-type contracts (DFARS), foreign-ownership limits,
  and the ban on awards to federal employees (FAR 3.601).
- **Definitive responsibility criteria**: specific, objective
  solicitation standards (e.g., "5 years' experience," "three completed
  projects of similar scope") — must be expressly in the solicitation;
  informational requests aren't definitive criteria (*Nilson Van*).
- **Small business + nonresponsibility = SBA COC referral** (FAR
  19.6): if SBA issues a Certificate of Competency the CO must award.
  Nonresponsibility >SAT based on performance/integrity gets documented
  in FAPIIS within 3 days (if no COC); DoD also copies the suspension
  & debarment official.
- Army: DORA bot (AFARS 5109.103) pulls SAM/FAPIIS automatically.
- **Review**: GAO reviews *nonresponsibility* for reasonableness but
  won't touch *affirmative* determinations except (1) unmet definitive
  criteria or (2) ignored information on "very serious matters" —
  criminal activity, massive scandal (*Sierra7*; *FCi Federal*
  sustained). GAO also won't review COC decisions absent bad faith,
  SBA regulatory violations, or vital info withheld from SBA. **COFC
  reviews everything** for rational basis (*Impresa Garufi*, 238 F.3d
  1324).

## Timeliness
### Publicizing (RFO FAR Part 5)
- Presolicitation notice on SAM.gov required >$20K (10 days for
  $20-25K; 15 days above $25K; commercial >SAT combines with the
  solicitation). Response times vary with WTO GPA/FTA coverage —
  the not-in-annual-forecast trade-agreement cases stretch to 40 days;
  noncommercial default is 30 days from solicitation; R&D 45.
- Award synopses >$25K (trade-agreement-covered or subcontracting-
  likely); public announcement coordination for awards >$5.5M (5 p.m.
  ET rule).

### Late is late (FAR 14.304 / 15.208; RFO 14.301 / 15.107)
- A late proposal "is tantamount to no proposal at all" (*Labatt*, 577
  F.3d 1375). Default receipt time if unstated: 4:30 p.m. local.
  Subcontractor/courier failures are the offeror's problem (*Kratos*).
- **Regulatory exceptions** (all require: received before award + no
  undue delay):
  1. **Electronic commerce** — received at the Government's initial
     point of entry by 5 p.m. one working day prior.
  2. **Government control** — proposal physically relinquished and in
     Government hands before the deadline. GAO refuses to apply this
     to *electronic* submissions (*Guidehouse*; *Sea Box*); **COFC
     disagrees** and protects emails stuck in Government servers
     (*Insight Systems*; *eSimplicity*) — forum choice matters.
  3. **Only proposal received** (RFPs only, not IFBs).
  Plus: RFO 15.107(b)(2) lets the CO accept a late modification of an
  otherwise-successful proposal that favors the Government.
- **Judicial exceptions**: (a) **Government frustration** — the agency
  was the *paramount cause* of lateness of a hand-carried offer
  (misdirection, bad instructions); fails when the offeror cut it close
  (courier arriving with one minute to spare, *Lani Eko*; no visitor
  pre-registration, *B&S Transport*). (b) **Systemic failure** — agency
  loses/misroutes proposals it actually received (*S.D.M. Supply*);
  requires more than occasional negligence (*Attainx*).
- **Emergency/unanticipated event**: deadline auto-extends when normal
  Government processes are interrupted (email storm counted,
  *Watterson*, 98 Fed. Cl. 84) — but not when the office stayed open
  and others delivered (*Hunter Contracting*).
- **Post-deadline extensions**: GAO tolerates amendments extending a
  closed deadline "to enhance competition" (*Geo-Seis I*); **COFC
  flatly rejects it** (*Geo-Seis II*, 77 Fed. Cl. 633; *Syncon*, 154
  Fed. Cl. 442 — it "renders the late-is-late rule a nullity"). No CAFC
  resolution yet — another forum-shopping fault line.

## Organizational conflicts of interest (FAR 9.5)
- Definition (FAR 2.101): other activities/relationships impair
  impartial advice, impair objectivity, or give an **unfair
  competitive advantage**. The agency decides; GAO defers absent
  "hard facts" showing arbitrariness (*QinetiQ*). Suspicion and
  innuendo lose; documented investigations win.
- **Three classic types**:
  1. **Unequal access to non-public information** (FAR 9.505-4) —
     access alone suffices, use is presumed once access is shown
     (*TeleCommunication Sys.*). Information must be real, substantial,
     competitively useful, non-public (need not be proprietary, *C2C*).
     Incumbency advantage alone is NOT an OCI (*Crowley*; *ARINC* —
     must be "so embedded" as to exceed normal incumbency).
     Mitigation: **firewalls work here**, or disclose the info to all.
  2. **Impaired objectivity** (FAR 9.505-3) — contractor's judgment
     under one contract touches its own interests (evaluating itself,
     recommending work it would then perform — *Steel Point*).
     Firewalls are "virtually irrelevant" (*Nortel*) — mitigate by
     carving out conflicted work to a firewalled sub or the agency.
     Agency final-approval authority doesn't cure it (*Inquiries*).
  3. **Biased ground rules** (FAR 9.505-1/-2) — helped write the SOW/
     specs/criteria for a competition it enters. No foreseeability
     caveat (*Energy Systems Group*). Hardest to mitigate — the harm
     is done; recusal or non-bid is the usual answer. Exceptions:
     sole source, design/development contractors, multiple firms
     helped draft.
- **Former Government employees**: hiring one with source-selection
  access is now analyzed under **FAR 3.1** (unfair competitive
  advantage), not 9.5 — same test, but **no waiver available**.
  Appearance of impropriety on hard facts is enough to disqualify
  (*NKF Engineering*; *Raytheon v. U.S.*, 170 Fed. Cl. 561; *CACI*).
  Investigation checklist (*Sigmatech*): access? shared? used in the
  proposal? competitively useful? Robust investigations survive review
  (CACI's CO reviewed 442 pages of interviews/emails).
- **Process duties**: identify early (FAR 9.504); consider the
  offeror's mitigation plan before excluding (*AT&T Gov't Solutions*);
  evaluate all offerors' OCIs evenhandedly; **waiver** available for
  9.5 conflicts (FAR 9.503) with a documented benefit-outweighs-risk
  rationale (*Analysis Group*).
- **MDAP overlay** (DFARS 209.571, WSARA): approved mitigation plans go
  INTO the contract; SETA contractors on MDAPs/pre-MDAPs are barred
  from developing/constructing the weapon system (non-waivable; narrow
  HCA domain-expertise exception).
- Losing at GAO doesn't end it — COFC protest jurisdiction is
  independent ("two bites"), and GAO decisions don't bind COFC.

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- Forum choice is outcome-determinative twice in this chapter:
  electronic late proposals (GAO kills them, COFC may save them) and
  post-deadline extensions (GAO allows, COFC forbids).
- For offerors: submit electronically a day early — the e-commerce
  exception only protects receipts by 5 p.m. the prior working day.
- Hiring a former Government employee for proposal work is the modern
  OCI minefield — and because it's FAR 3.1, the agency can't waive it.
- Firewalls fix unequal-access but never impaired-objectivity; match
  the mitigation to the OCI type or the plan fails review.
