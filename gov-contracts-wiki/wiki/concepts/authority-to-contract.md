# Authority to Contract

**One-line:** Only agents with actual authority bind the United States —
apparent authority never does — so every "who told you that?" question
matters, and unauthorized commitments are payable only through
ratification or its fallbacks.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 4 (whole
chapter).*

## Contract formation basics
- Elements: mutual intent, offer/acceptance, consideration, **and an
  official with actual authority** (*Allen Orchards*, 749 F.2d 1571).
- Writing generally required (31 U.S.C. § 1501(a)(1)); oral deals can
  survive if documentary "additional facts" show a meeting of the minds
  (*Narva Harris*, 574 F.2d 508).
- Implied-in-**fact** contracts = same elements, inferred from conduct;
  implied-in-**law** contracts are outside CDA/Tucker Act jurisdiction
  entirely (*Hercules*, 516 U.S. 417).

## Who has authority
- FAR vests contracting authority in agency heads (RFO FAR 1.401), who
  delegate to HCAs (COs by virtue of position) → COs appointed in
  writing on **SF 1402**, bound by the warrant's limits.
- **CORs/COTRs** get specific technical/administrative functions only —
  standard designations expressly exclude anything obligating money.
- Non-FAR instruments (OTs under 10 U.S.C. § 4021) need properly
  appointed **Agreements Officers**.

## The iron rule and its softeners
- **Actual authority only**: *Federal Crop Ins. v. Merrill*, 332 U.S.
  380 — the Government is never bound by apparent authority (though
  contractors ARE bound by their agents' apparent authority, *American
  Anchor*, 331 F.2d 860).
- Theories that can still bind the Government:
  1. **Implied actual authority** — contracting power integral to
     assigned duties the employee can't perform without it (*H. Landau*,
     886 F.2d 322; QAR ordering testing, *Switlik*) — but never where
     the contract reserves changes to the CO (*Winter v. Cath-dr/
     Balti*, 497 F.3d 1339).
  2. **Ratification** — express (FAR RFO 1.405) or implied, where the
     CO knows of the commitment and adopts it (requesting payment,
     *Reliable Disposal*; sixteen months of institutional acceptance,
     *Digicon*, 56 Fed. Cl. 425).
  3. **Imputed knowledge** — inspector/COR knowledge charged to the CO
     when the relationship presumes reporting (*Leiden*).
  4. **Equitable estoppel** — post-*Mabus* three-part test (misleading
     conduct, reliance, material prejudice), and against the Government
     requires **affirmative misconduct** (*Zacharin*, 213 F.3d 1366) —
     nearly impossible.

## Unauthorized commitments & ratification (FAR RFO 1.405)
Ratification requires ALL of: benefit received/accepted; ratifying
official had (and still has) the authority; the contract would otherwise
have been proper; fair and reasonable price; CO recommends payment with
legal concurrence; funds were and are available; within agency limits.
Army delegations: SCO/PARC ≤$100K; chief of contracting office ≤$10K
(AFARS 5101.602-3(b)(3)).

If ratification is refused:
1. **Extraordinary contractual relief** (FAR Part 50 / P.L. 85-804) —
   formalizing informal commitments made in good-faith reliance.
2. **Quantum meruit** doubtful-claims settlement (31 U.S.C. § 3702 —
   DOHA Claims Division for DoD): permissible procurement + benefit
   accepted + good faith + reasonable value.
3. **CDA claim** on an implied-in-fact contract theory.

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- Ask for the warrant (SF 1402) or COR designation letter before
  relying on anyone's direction — "the Government told me to" is only
  as good as the teller's authority.
- Contractors: route everything money-touching through the CO in
  writing; the estoppel path against the Government is a dead end.
