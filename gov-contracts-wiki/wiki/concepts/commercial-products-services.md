# Commercial Products & Commercial Services (FAR Part 12)

**One-line:** If the marketplace already sells it, buy it like the
marketplace does: mandatory Part 12 procedures, fixed-price preference,
a short commercial clause set, and — post-2026 RFO — sticky
commerciality determinations that bind later buys.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 11 (whole
chapter). Note: the chapter is built around the RFO FAR Part 12 /
DFARS 212 class deviation effective Feb 1, 2026 (DARS 2026-00028).*

## Definitions (FAR 2.101, split by FY19 NDAA § 836 per the 809 Panel)
- **Commercial product**: any product *other than real property* that
  (1) has been sold/leased/licensed — or merely **offered** — to the
  general public (*Coherent* — actual sales not required); (2) evolved
  from such a product and will exist in time for delivery; (3) is such
  a product with **customary** modifications, or **minor** Government-
  unique modifications (don't significantly alter non-governmental
  function/essential characteristics — *Canberra*; dollar values are
  guideposts, not conclusive; see the KC-767 tanker controversy);
  (4) customary combinations; (5) intra-affiliate transfers; or
  (6) qualifying **NDIs** developed at private expense and sold in
  substantial quantities to multiple state/local/foreign governments
  (major redesign work flunks NDI, *Trimble Navigation*).
- **Commercial service**: (a) installation/maintenance/repair/training
  in support of a commercial product, if the source sells similar
  services to the public on similar terms; or (b) services **including
  construction** sold competitively in substantial quantities at
  catalog or market prices for specific tasks/outcomes — the RFO's
  explicit inclusion of construction reverses the old 2003 OFPP
  anti-Part-12-construction guidance (a finished building is still real
  property, so never a commercial *product*).
- **COTS**: subset of commercial products — sold in substantial
  quantities AND offered **without modification** (equipment that would
  only become commercial through this buy doesn't count, *Chant
  Engineering*).
- Commerciality determinations are discretionary agency calls; GAO
  reviews only for reasonableness (*Precision Lift*).

## General rules
- **Part 12 is mandatory** when the item meets the definition (FAR
  12.102; *Palantir v. U.S.*, 904 F.3d 980 (Fed. Cir. 2018) — agencies
  must actually do the market research before defaulting to
  developmental buys). Exceptions: at/below the MPT, SF 44, imprest
  funds, GPC-as-purchase-method, direct interagency acquisitions.
- Check **required sources** (RFO FAR Part 8) first; Part 12 policies
  trump inconsistent policies elsewhere in the FAR.
- **DoD commerciality determinations >SAT** (RFO DFARS 212.001-70):
  a prior DoD determination is **presumed valid** for later buys of the
  same item — overturning it takes an HCA written determination. No
  prior one? CO writes a new one (one-level-up approval for the softer
  definitional prongs), uploads it to the DoD Commercial Item Database
  within 30 days, and must search that database first.
- **IT >SAT must be commercial** unless the HCA determines in writing
  that no commercial product/service is suitable (RFO DFARS 212.172).
- Primes/subs must incorporate commercial/NDI components to the
  maximum extent practicable.

## Contract types
- **FFP or FP/EPA required** as the default (RFO FAR 12.207(a)).
- **T&M/LH for commercial services** only with a D&F that no other
  type is suitable: describe market research, show the work can't be
  reasonably estimated at award, structure the requirement to maximize
  fixed pricing, and plan to migrate future buys to fixed price. The
  RFO **removed** the old, more restrictive DoD-specific T&M limits —
  one government-wide rule now (RFO FAR 12.207(b)).
- Indefinite-delivery vehicles OK if orders are FFP/FP-EPA (or T&M/LH
  for services). Incentives allowed but must key on factors **other
  than cost**. Cost-reimbursement is prohibited for commercial buys.

## Competition & evaluation (streamlined, not lawless)
- **Combined synopsis/solicitation** (FAR 12.603): one document, no
  separate 15-day synopsis period — response time just has to be
  reasonable for the complexity/urgency.
- Evaluation can be as thin as **technical capability, price, past
  performance** (52.212-2), judged against the need rather than
  pre-set subfactors.
- **No certified cost or pricing data** — but the CO must still find
  the price fair and reasonable, escalating from market research →
  recent Government/commercial purchase prices → uncertified cost data
  (10 U.S.C. § 3453(d); RFO DFARS 212.204).
- DoD adds (RFO DFARS 212, eff. 2026): mandatory **supply-chain-risk
  evaluation factor for all IT** buys; the LPTA limits of DFARS
  215.101-2-70 apply to commercial acquisitions; **one-offer**
  situations trigger the DFARS 215.371 sole-offer procedures; and the
  award rationale (including any tradeoff) must be documented.

## The commercial clause set & tailoring
- Five core instruments: **52.212-1** (instructions), **52.212-2**
  (evaluation), **52.212-3** (reps & certs), **52.212-4** (terms —
  inspection, acceptance, termination, warranties), **52.212-5** (the
  pass-down clause incorporating only statutorily required extras).
  RFO DFARS Tables 212.2/212.3/212.4 list the DoD add-ons (NIST
  800-171 notice, CMMC, Buy American, whistleblower rights…);
  an omnibus-clause deviation automates selection in PD2.
- **Tailoring** of 52.212-1/-4 via addenda is allowed after market
  research — but NEVER these 52.212-4 paragraphs: assignments,
  disputes, payment, invoice, other compliances, compliance with laws
  unique to Government contracts, unauthorized obligations. Anything
  **inconsistent with customary commercial practice** needs a waiver
  (HCA for DoD).
- Tailoring sweet spots: acceptance/inspection for critical items,
  termination (Part 49 is guidance only — commercial T4C pays a
  percentage of price, not settlement costs), **warranties** (demand at
  least what the public gets), and technical data/software (take the
  standard commercial license unless it conflicts with federal law —
  see [Intellectual Property](intellectual-property.md)).

## Why it looks this way: the Section 809 Panel
FY16 NDAA panel (2016-2019, 98 recommendations) pushed splitting
"commercial item" into product/service and lowering barriers for
commercial firms; the 2026 RFO is the direct implementation of that
vision. Useful context when older case law or guidance uses
"commercial item."

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- "Offered to the public" is enough — a product with zero sales can
  still be commercial; conversely "we'll make it commercial via this
  contract" fails COTS.
- The prior-determination presumption cuts both ways: it speeds your
  buy, but undoing a bad legacy determination now requires the HCA.
- Watch the "minor modification" prong — it's where commerciality
  fights (and audit findings like the KC-767) actually happen.
- Commercial T4C ≠ FAR Part 49 termination: different clause,
  different settlement math; don't cite Part 49 rights you don't have.
