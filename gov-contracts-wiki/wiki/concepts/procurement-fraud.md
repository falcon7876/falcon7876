# Procurement Fraud

**One-line:** Fraud freezes the contract machinery — the CO can't
settle or pay a tainted claim — and triggers four coordinated remedy
tracks: criminal (DOJ), civil FCA, administrative (suspension/
debarment, AFCA), and contractual.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 24 (whole
chapter).*

## Spotting it
- **Pre-award schemes**: bribery/kickbacks (Anti-Kickback Act, 41
  U.S.C. §§ 8701-07), **bid rigging** (Sherman Act § 1 — patterns of
  winners, losers becoming subs, near-identical bids), defective
  pricing as fraud (TINA predicate, *Broderson*), and **fraudulent
  sole-sourcing** (tailored specs, vague J&As, values suspiciously just
  under approval thresholds, split purchases).
- **Post-award schemes**: **product substitution/defective testing**
  (look-alike goods, missing source docs, counterfeit parts — 18
  U.S.C. § 2320 criminalizes trafficking counterfeit *military* goods;
  report suspects to GIDEP) and **false invoices** (altered supporting
  docs, goods that can't be located, inflated cost billings —
  including *implied certification* of contract compliance, *Shaw v.
  AAA Engineering*).

## The moment fraud surfaces
- **Stop everything.** 41 U.S.C. § 7103(c)/FAR 33.210(b): no CO or
  agency head may settle, pay, compromise, or adjust **any claim
  involving fraud**. "Denial" of a fraudulent claim really means
  doing nothing while remedies proceed; CO refers to the fraud
  investigators (FAR 33.209).
- Army pipeline (AR 27-40 ch. 8): Procurement Fraud Advisor consults
  OTJAG's Procurement Fraud Division → CID report → **flash report**
  → referral to the debarring official → comprehensive **remedies
  plan** weighing all four remedy types. DoDI 7050.05 requires
  centralized monitoring of "significant" cases (≥$500K loss, any
  corruption, any product substitution).
- **Contractor mandatory disclosure** (FAR 3.1003, since 2008):
  knowing failure by a principal to timely disclose credible evidence
  of Title 18 fraud/bribery/gratuity violations, civil FCA violations,
  or **significant overpayments** is itself a suspension/debarment
  cause — until 3 years after final payment. Contracts >$7.5M/120
  days add 52.203-13: written code of ethics in 30 days, compliance
  program + internal control system in 90, disclosures to the agency
  IG. Hotline posters >$6M (52.203-14); whistleblower reprisal
  protections for contractor employees (10 U.S.C. § 4701; FAR 3.9,
  52.203-17).

## Criminal remedies (DOJ leads)
Conspiracy (18 U.S.C. §§ 286, 371); **criminal false claims** (§ 287 —
payment not required, *Coachman*); **false statements** (§ 1001 —
oral/unsworn counts, materiality = natural tendency to influence);
mail/wire fraud (§§ 1341-43); **Major Fraud Act** (§ 1031 — ≥$1M
procurements, fines to $10M per prosecution); Sherman Act bid rigging
(corporate fines to $100M); UCMJ articles for servicemembers (Art. 124
fraud, 107 false official statements, 121 larceny...).

## Civil False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. §§ 3729-33)
- Liability for knowingly presenting false claims, using false
  records, or conspiring; FERA (2009) closed the *Allison Engine*
  subcontractor gap — claims to primes count.
- **Elements**: *scienter* (actual knowledge, deliberate ignorance, or
  reckless disregard — judged **subjectively**, *Schutte v. Supervalu*
  (2023)); presentment; claim (any request where the Government
  provided even a portion of the money, *Wisconsin Bell* (2025));
  **materiality** (*Escobar* factors — condition-of-payment label not
  dispositive, essence of the bargain, Government's past payment
  behavior; "guards who can't shoot straight," *Triple Canopy*);
  falsity (facially false, express/implied certification, fraud in the
  inducement, actionable half-truths).
- **Damages**: treble + per-claim penalties (inflation-adjusted:
  ~$14,308-$28,608) — automatic per claim, no proof of damages or
  payment needed; cooperation/self-disclosure can mitigate multipliers
  (Justice Manual 4-4.112). Limitations: 6 years, or 3 from official
  knowledge, max 10 (*Cochise v. Hunt*).
- **Qui tam**: relators sue in the Government's name; complaint under
  seal 60 days (extensions routine); relator's share 15-25% (Government
  intervenes) or 25-30% (relator alone). **Public disclosure bar**
  unless the relator is an original source; Government may dismiss over
  the relator's objection after a hearing.
- **Special plea in fraud** (28 U.S.C. § 2514, COFC only): forfeiture
  of the ENTIRE claim of anyone who practiced fraud in proving it.

## Administrative remedies
- **Administrative FCA** (31 U.S.C. §§ 3801-12; renamed and revamped
  from PFCRA by the FY25 NDAA): agency-level remedy for false claims/
  statements up to **$1M per related-claims group** (up from $150K);
  now covers reverse false claims. DOJ must approve the complaint
  (90-day review) and gets 30-day notice of settlements; hearings can
  go to a **board of contract appeals** if no presiding officer;
  penalties ~$14,308/claim + assessment ≤2× the claim; preponderance
  standard; **no qui tam**.
- **Suspension & debarment** (FAR 9.4): protection, never punishment
  (FAR 9.402). Debarment causes: conviction/civil judgment (fraud,
  antitrust, embezzlement, false "Made in America" labels, § 847
  violations) or preponderance findings (serious performance failures,
  >$10K delinquent taxes, mandatory-disclosure failures, any cause
  affecting present responsibility). Suspension needs only **adequate
  evidence** — an indictment suffices. Effect: government-wide
  exclusion; existing contracts continue but no new orders beyond
  minimums, no options, no extensions absent compelling-reason
  determinations. Debarment generally ≤3 years; suspension is
  temporary (12 months, +6 on AAG request, 18 max without legal
  proceedings). Softer tools: requests for information, show-cause
  letters, **administrative agreements** (FAPIIS-posted remediation
  deals), informal letter agreements.

## Contract remedies (the CO's lane)
Refuse/withhold payment on reasonable suspicion (44 Comp. Gen. 111);
**suspend progress payments** on agency-head "substantial evidence"
determination (FAR 32.006 — but get the written determination first:
skipping it was a Government breach, *TRS Research*); terminate
settlement negotiations (FAR 49.106); nonresponsibility; defective-
pricing price reductions (52.215-10); profit-recapture for PIA
violations (52.203-10); **T4D for fraud** — even fraud discovered
after the termination supports it (*Joseph Morton*); **void/rescind**
under FAR 3.7 (final conviction or agency-head PIA finding) or the
**Gratuities clause** (52.203-3 — DoD-unique, 3-10× exemplary
damages, the Fuerth bidding-scheme cases).

## Fraud at the boards
Boards lack jurisdiction over fraud-tainted appeals (COs can't decide
them, 41 U.S.C. § 7103(a)); dismissal motions require more than bare
allegations (*Triax*; *General Construction*). CDA **fraud
counterclaims** (§ 7103(c)(2): unsupported-claim amount + review
costs) run only in the COFC — boards say DOJ alone can assert them
(*TDC Management*); COFC enforces them (*UMC Electronics*; *Railway
Logistics*).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The instant fraud is suspected, the CO's settlement authority ends —
  paying or compromising a suspect claim is itself a violation.
- Coordinate before acting: a hasty contract remedy (like T4D) can
  complicate DOJ's criminal case; the remedies plan exists to sequence
  the four tracks.
- Suspension on indictment alone is lawful — contractors facing
  charges should engage the SDO early (present responsibility,
  remediation, administrative agreement).
- FCA exposure math is brutal: per-invoice penalties stack even with
  zero damages; mandatory-disclosure failures add S&D exposure for
  three years after final payment.
- The FY25 NDAA's AFCA makes $1M-and-under fraud administratively
  actionable — expect more agency-level cases that never reach DOJ
  civil litigation.
