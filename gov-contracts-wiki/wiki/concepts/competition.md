# Competition (CICA)

**One-line:** CICA requires full and open competition to the maximum
extent practicable; every departure needs a statutory exception, a
justification with teeth, and — the case law's favorite trap — cannot be
excused by the agency's own failure to plan ahead.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 6 (whole
chapter).*

## Three levels of competition (RFO FAR Part 6)
1. **Full and open** (6.101): all responsible sources may compete —
   opportunity, not actual multiple offers, is the standard. Vehicles:
   sealed bids, competitive proposals, combinations, and "other
   competitive procedures" (FSS, BAAs, CSOs, prize competitions).
   Incumbency advantage alone isn't unfair (*Assured Performance*).
2. **Full and open after exclusion of sources** (6.102): (a)
   alternative-source exclusions via agency-head D&F (dual-sourcing,
   mobilization base, high-demand, medical/safety needs); (b) **small
   business set-asides** (no D&F needed — but competition among ALL
   eligible smalls); (c) local-firm set-asides in disasters (42 U.S.C.
   § 5150).
3. **Other than full and open** (6.103): the seven statutory
   exceptions.

## The seven sole-source exceptions (10 U.S.C. § 3204 / 41 U.S.C. § 3304)
1. **Only one responsible source** — DoD may use "one or a limited
   number"; must still notice intentions so others can demonstrate
   capability (*Lockheed Martin—Owego*); covers follow-on major-system
   contracts where switching means substantial duplicated cost or
   unacceptable delay; brand-name justifications live here (FAR
   6.103-1(d) + 11.204).
2. **Unusual and compelling urgency** — the "bridge contract"
   workhorse. J&A may follow award (30 days); still must solicit as
   many sources as practicable (*Bausch & Lomb*); buy only the minimum
   quantity (*Signals & Sys.*); PoP limited to the time needed to
   compete a successor, max 1 year absent head-of-agency exception.
3. **Industrial mobilization / essential R&D capability / expert
   services** (incl. litigation experts and neutrals).
4. **International agreement** (SOFAs, FMS written direction).
5. **Authorized or required by statute** (FPI, AbilityOne, 8(a) and
   other SBA sole-source authorities, GPO) — usually no J&A (8(a) over
   $30M/$100M DoD excepted).
6. **National security** (disclosure would compromise it — mere
   classification isn't enough).
7. **Public interest** — agency-head individual D&F + 30-day
   congressional notice.

**Never valid**: lack of advance planning or funding-expiration concerns
(§ 3204(e)(5)) — GAO sustains protests where agencies drifted into sole
source (*RBC Bearings*; *Major Contracting*; *Heros* — "agencies must act
affirmatively to obtain and safeguard competition").

## J&A mechanics (RFO FAR 6.104)
Stand-alone, certified-accurate document: exception cited, why the
contractor is unique, sources-sought efforts, fair-and-reasonable price
determination, market research, steps to remove barriers next time.
Approval tiers (civilian/DoD): CO ≤$900K/$10M; competition advocate to
$20M/$100M; HCA to $90M/$500M; SPE above. Post to the GPE (14 days
post-award; 30 for urgency) and keep up 30 days. Amend before award if
value, strategy, or requirements change.

## Innovation lanes that count as competitive procedures
- **BAAs** (RFO FAR 35.016): basic/applied research not tied to a
  specific system; peer/scientific review; proposals not compared
  against each other; RDT&E-funded; no Part 15 rules or debriefing-
  based protest timeliness (*Millennium Space*). 10 U.S.C. § 4004 lets
  DoD add limited prototype/initial-production line items (≤2 years,
  ≤$100M).
- **CSOs** (10 U.S.C. § 3458; DFARS 212.70): BAA-style general
  solicitation for commercial products/services/NDIs (FY26 NDAA dropped
  the "innovative" qualifier and added follow-on production authority);
  fixed-price only; SPE approval >$100M; treated as commercial buys.
- Also exempt from Part 6: simplified acquisitions (competition "to the
  maximum extent practicable" — no splitting requirements to stay under
  the SAT), A&E under Brooks Act, healthcare personal services, prize
  competitions.

## Where competition questions hide in existing contracts
- **In-scope mods & evaluated options** are exempt (FAR 6.001(c)); an
  unevaluated/unpriced option (incl. 52.217-8 extensions) needs Part 6
  compliance (*Major Contracting*, B-401472.2). See
  [Contract Changes](contract-changes.md) for the scope test.
- **Orders under ID/IQs**: exempt from Part 6 but governed by fair-
  opportunity tiers (see [Contract Types](contract-types.md)); orders
  exceeding contract scope/period/ceiling revert to Part 6.
- **Reprocurements after T4D**: any reasonable method, competition to
  the maximum extent practicable; short gap since original competition
  justifies going to the next-in-line offeror (*Maersk Line*).
- **No-cost contracts**: CICA applies to civilian agencies but NOT
  military ones; the FAR applies to neither (no appropriated funds).

## Implementation duties
- **Competition advocates** required agency-wide and per activity
  (41 U.S.C. § 1705) — but complaining to one doesn't toll protest
  deadlines (*Allied-Signal*).
- **Acquisition planning** (FAR Part 7; DFARS written-plan thresholds
  $10M development / $50M production) and **market research** (Part 10)
  precede every acquisition over the SAT.
- **Specifications** (Part 11): state needs as functions/performance;
  restrictive terms only for minimum needs. Recurring protest grounds:
  specs written around one product, geographic limits, exceeding
  minimum needs, brand-name-or-equal without salient characteristics
  (cancellation required, *CAMSS Shelters*), improper bundling, and
  ambiguity destroying the common basis for competition.

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- "We ran out of time" is never a J&A — the urgency exception fails
  when the urgency is self-inflicted.
- Buy the minimum under urgency; bridge contracts sized or timed
  generously invite sustained protests.
- Set-asides need no J&A, which makes them the path of least resistance
  when eligible smalls exist.
