# Contract Disputes Act (Claims & Appeals)

**One-line:** The exclusive process for post-award contract disputes:
a proper written claim to the CO → CO final decision (or deemed denial)
→ binding forum election between the board (90 days) and COFC (12
months), both de novo, with CDA interest running from claim receipt.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook chs. 19A & 19B
(whole chapters).*

## Scope & the protest/dispute line
- CDA (41 U.S.C. §§ 7101-7109) covers express and implied-in-fact
  contracts for property, services, construction, and disposal of
  personal property. Not covered: independent torts, fraud claims,
  penalties other agencies administer, **bid protests** (formation
  fights → [Bid Protests](bid-protests.md); administration fights →
  CDA). NAF contracts: only the exchanges, post-*Slattery* questions
  open (*Minesen*).
- Disputes clause 52.233-1: continue performing "arising under" the
  contract disputes; Alt I extends the duty to "relating to" (DoD uses
  for mission-critical work).

## What makes a valid claim (where most cases die)
1. **Written** demand seeking, as a matter of right, (a) a **sum
   certain**, (b) adjustment/interpretation of terms, or (c) other
   relief. Routine invoices aren't claims until disputed or ignored
   unreasonably. An REA can be a claim if it (implicitly) requests a
   final decision (*Zafer*, 40 F.4th 1365; *Reflectone*, 60 F.3d 1572).
2. **Submitted to the CO** with an express or implied request for final
   decision.
3. **Certified if >$100,000** (good faith / accurate data / amount
   reflects liability / authorized signer — exact text in 52.233-1(d)).
   - Aggregate related increases/decreases; claims from a "common or
     related set of operative facts" are ONE claim — no splitting to
     duck certification (*Placeway*; *K-Con*, 778 F.3d 1000).
   - **No certification = no claim** (not curable); *defective*
     certification (substantial compliance attempted) IS curable before
     judgment. Fraud-tainted or reckless certifications aren't.
   - Sum certain is a claim-processing rule, not jurisdictional (*ECC
     Int'l*, 79 F.4th 1364) — but the Government waives it by sitting on
     the objection (*JE Dunn*).
   - Enlarged-claim doctrine: amounts can grow on appeal if based on
     the same operative facts and not reasonably knowable earlier.
4. **Within 6 years of accrual** (all events fixing liability known or
   knowable + some injury). Not jurisdictional (*Sikorsky*, 773 F.3d
   1315), so it's an affirmative defense; equitable tolling possible.
- Supporting cost data is NOT a jurisdictional prerequisite (*H.L.
  Smith*, 49 F.3d 1563).
- Proper claimants: prime contractors only. Subs need **sponsored/
  pass-through claims** (watch the *Severin* doctrine — prime must have
  at least contingent liability to the sub). Sureties only after a
  takeover agreement.

## CO final decisions
- Due in **60 days** (claims ≤$100K, if decision requested); >$100K
  certified: decide in 60 days or give a **firm date** ("at least
  another 60 days" isn't one). Failure = **deemed denial**, appealable;
  tribunal can also order a decision. Deemed denial never starts the
  appeal clock.
- Must be written, describe the dispute, state the rationale, and give
  the **exact appeal-rights advisory** (90 days board / 12 months
  COFC); defective advisories can toll the clock on detrimental
  reliance (*Decker*, 76 F.3d 1573).
- Must be the CO's **independent act** (advisors fine; dictation not —
  *Climatic Rainwear*). Deliver with proof of receipt — the 90-day
  clock runs from contractor receipt, and the Government loses when it
  can't prove it (*Riley & Ephriam*, 408 F.3d 1369).
- Finality evaporates easily: meeting with the contractor to discuss
  the merits can "reconsider" the decision (*Sach Sinha*); restarting
  the clock requires a NEW final decision.
- **Interest** runs from CO receipt of the (even defectively certified)
  claim — simple, Treasury rate, huge on big claims (*Servidone*:
  accrues even on costs not yet incurred).

## Government claims
- Also require a CO final decision (T4D notices, withholding/setoff,
  CAS determinations are immediately appealable Government claims). No
  certification. Two-step practice: demand letter, then final decision.
  6-year limit too (except fraud). Interest from initial demand.
- Contractor defenses that "effectively request a change in the
  contract" (time extension, adjustment) must themselves be submitted
  as claims first (*Maropakis*, 609 F.3d 1323); pure defenses like
  prior material breach need not (*Laguna*, 828 F.3d 1364).
- Fraud counterclaims go straight to COFC — no CO decision needed
  (*Simko*); COs have no authority over suspected-fraud claims.

## Forum choice (binding election)
| | ASBCA (boards) | COFC |
|---|---|---|
| Deadline | **90 days** from receipt (jurisdictional, no waiver — *Cosmic Constr.*) | **12 months** (one day late = dead, *White Buffalo*) |
| Review | De novo; final decision gets no weight (*Wilner*) | De novo |
| Counsel | Not required (officers may appear) | Attorney required; DOJ defends, agency loses control |
| Fast tracks | Expedited ≤$50K (or ≤$150K small biz, 120 days, no appeal); accelerated ≤$100K (180 days) | none |
| Appeal | CAFC, 120 days | CAFC, 60 days |
- Election is binding once filed in a forum with jurisdiction. Board
  decisions don't bind COFC. Boards apply FRE loosely, build the
  record via the **Rule 4 file** (Government: 30 days from NOA), and
  construe notices of appeal liberally (writing + dissatisfaction +
  intent to appeal + timely). U.S. Mail gets mailed-date credit at
  ASBCA; commercial couriers don't.
- **Fulford doctrine**: a timely appeal of excess reprocurement costs
  reopens the underlying T4D even if the T4D appeal window was missed.
- Remedies: money (Judgment Fund pays; agency repays with current
  funds), declaratory relief, EAJA fees (board: 5 U.S.C. § 504; COFC:
  28 U.S.C. § 2412) — but no specific performance or injunctions.
- T4C settlement proposals are non-routine but not claims until
  **impasse** (*Ellett*, 93 F.3d 1537) — no CDA interest until then.

## Litigation mechanics at COFC (ch. 19B)
- Agency duties the moment litigation is anticipated: **litigation
  hold** (suspend document destruction, preserve ESI), call DOJ before
  the § 520 call letter arrives, then produce the **litigation report**
  (facts, draft answer, memo of law, witnesses, exhibits; attorney
  work product).
- Government answer/motion due 60 days; JPSR at 49 days; discovery
  mirrors FRCP with real teeth — *Mortenson*: $22M sanction; *4DD
  Holdings*: spoliation adverse inference. Use FRE 502(d) clawback
  orders for privilege protection.
- Settlement authority sits with DOJ once in litigation (28 U.S.C.
  § 516); agency recommends. Offers of judgment (RCFC 68) can shift
  costs and cap EAJA exposure. ADR available via settlement judge or
  mini-trial (RCFC App. H).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The 90-day board clock is merciless and jurisdictional; the 12-month
  COFC clock nearly so. Calendar both the moment a final decision
  arrives — and for the Government, prove delivery or lose the clock.
- Certify early and correctly: "no certification" kills the claim;
  "defective certification" survives. Know which side of that line a
  submission sits on.
- CO conduct after a final decision matters — a courtesy meeting can
  reopen everything.
