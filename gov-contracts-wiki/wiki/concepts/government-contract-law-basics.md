# Government Contract Law — Foundations

**One-line:** Government contracting borrows commercial contract law
only until a statute or regulation displaces it — which is nearly
always — so the practitioner's first question is never "what did the
parties agree to" but "what does the statute/FAR require."

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 1 (whole
chapter).*

## Commercial law vs. government contract law
- When the Government "enters the domain of commerce, it submits
  itself to the same laws that govern individuals there" (*Cooke v.
  United States*, 91 U.S. 389 (1875)) — commercial law is the
  backdrop.
- But statutes and regulations preempt it almost everywhere, and the
  contractor bears the risk of knowing them: "the person entering
  into such a contract must look to the statute under which it is
  made, and see for himself that his contract comes within the terms
  of the law" (*The Floyd Acceptances*, 74 U.S. 666 (1868)) — the
  same root as the actual-authority doctrine in [Authority to
  Contract](authority-to-contract.md).

## Public policy in the contract — four clause rules
1. Clauses **required** by statute or regulation are read INTO the
   contract by operation of law (*G.L. Christian & Assocs.* — the
   **Christian doctrine**; regulations with statutory authority have
   the force of law).
2. Clauses included **in violation of** statute/regulation are read
   OUT (*Empresa de Viacao Terceirense*; a CO acting beyond actual
   authority doesn't bind the Government).
3. Erroneously incorporated clauses are **replaced** with the correct
   version (*S.J. Amoroso*).
4. Contracts tainted by **fraud in the inducement** are void ab
   initio — unratifiable, and the contractor recovers nothing
   (*Godley*; *Schuepferling*). See [Procurement
   Fraud](procurement-fraud.md).
- Note the NAF exception: the Christian doctrine does not reach NAFI
  contracts — see [Army NAF Contracting](army-naf-contracting.md).

## System objectives & the players
- Schooner's *Desiderata* core principles — **competition,
  transparency, integrity, fairness** — plus socioeconomic policy
  overlays and customer satisfaction; they tension against each other
  constantly (speed vs. competition, fairness vs. finality).
- The acquisition team spans commander/requiring activity/user,
  comptroller, contracting office, small-business and competition
  advocates, legal, contract administration, and DCAA on the
  Government side, mirrored by the contractor's management, finance,
  production, contracts, and counsel.
- **Contract attorney roles**: advisor (formation, administration,
  fiscal), litigator (protests, disputes, collateral proceedings),
  fraud fighter, and business counselor — counsel is part of the CO's
  team (RFO FAR 1.102; AFARS 5101.602-2 requires full participation
  from planning through closeout).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The Christian doctrine cuts against the drafter's silence: a
  missing mandatory clause (T4C, disputes) is in the contract anyway
  — but ONLY in FAR-governed procurements; OTs and NAF contracts get
  no such rescue.
- Commercial-law intuitions (course of dealing, apparent authority,
  UCC gap-fillers) are the most common source of bad advice in this
  field; check for the statute first.
- Early attorney involvement is the recurring theme of the whole
  Deskbook: formation mistakes surface as administration disputes
  years later.
