# Inspection, Acceptance & Warranty

**One-line:** The Government may inspect anything before acceptance —
reasonably — but once it accepts, finality attaches, and only latent
defects, fraud, gross mistake, or a warranty reopen the deal.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 16 (whole
chapter).*

## Inspection basics (RFO FAR Part 46; clauses 52.246-2 to -14)
- The inspection clauses are **remedy-granting clauses**; pick the one
  matching contract type and item (supply/service/construction, FP vs.
  cost). Three QA tiers: rely on contractor inspection, standard, and
  higher-level quality requirements.
- Contractors must maintain an adequate inspection system; the
  Government may inspect "at all places and times... before
  acceptance."
- **Testing discipline**: contract-specified tests are presumed
  reasonable; the Government may use other tests only if they impose no
  **more stringent standard** than the contract (*United Technologies/
  Sikorsky*). Unspecified tests should match intended use (*A-Nam Cong
  Ty* — can't test coastal barges on the high seas), use industry
  standards, and be performed correctly. Testing a commercial item
  beyond commercial practice = **constructive change** (*Max Blau*).
- The Government need not inspect at all, and its failure to catch a
  defect doesn't excuse the contractor (52.246-2(j)) — but it may not
  unreasonably refuse the contractor's own reasonable preliminary
  testing (*Alonso & Carus* — risk of loss shifted).
- **Costs**: contractor furnishes facilities/assistance at its plant;
  Government pays elsewhere; re-inspection after untimely tender or
  prior rejection is chargeable to the contractor. Construction
  destructive testing: defective → contractor pays; conforming →
  equitable adjustment + time.
- **First article testing** (RFO FAR 9.3): preproduction proof for new
  suppliers, changed processes, resumed production, or performance
  specs; solicitation must state the approval characteristics.

## Remedies under the inspection clause — timing matters
| When defects tendered | Government options |
|---|---|
| **Before** delivery date | Reject; require correction (reasonable opportunity); accept at reduced price. NO default termination yet |
| **On** delivery date | Same three, plus T4D — subject to substantial compliance/completion doctrines |
| **After** delivery date | Same three, plus T4D for late delivery |
- Price reductions measure the **contractor's cost savings**, not the
  Government's damages (*Federal Boiler*; *Blount Bros.*; *Valley
  Asphalt* — wrong-elevation runway, nominal reduction, no value loss).
- Contractor fails to correct after notice + opportunity: reprocure
  the correction commercially (charge the contractor), fix it in-house,
  keep at reduced price, or T4D.
- **Services**: normally allow re-performance at no fee; if
  re-performance is impossible, require future compliance + reduce
  price (random-sampling deduction schedules OK, *Teltara*). Split
  authority on whether deductions waive the right to also T4D for the
  same failures (*W.M. Grace* vs. *Cervetto*).

## Strict vs. substantial compliance
- Default rule: **strict compliance** with specs, even against trade
  practice (*R.B. Wright* — three coats of paint means three); slight
  defects are defects (*Mech-Con*).
- **Substantial compliance** (supply) / substantial completion
  (construction/services) buys the contractor cure time past the
  delivery date — elements: timely delivery, good-faith belief of
  conformity, minor curable defects, time not of the essence
  (*Radiation Technology*). It affects **when**, not whether, the
  Government may T4D (*Firma Tiefbau Meier*).
- **Economic waste**: the Government must accept noncompliant
  construction only when the work is suitable for its intended purpose
  (which itself requires substantial compliance) AND correction cost
  far exceeds the gain (*Granite Construction*).

## Unreasonable inspection = Government liability
Overzealous or shifting inspection standards support contractor claims
(equitable adjustment, delay, even breach): "overnight change" in
standards (*Gary Aircraft*), 100% mopping demanded where 10% specified
(*Harris Systems*), inspector megalomania (*Libertatia* — COR claiming
divinity), doubled inspection points (*Adams*). Improper inspection can
also excuse contractor delay and defeat a T4D (*Puma Chemical*).

**Waiver/estoppel**: repeated knowing acceptances of the same
nonconformity from the same contractor can waive the spec (*Gresham* —
dishwashers; *Astro Dynamics* — seven shipments), but passive
acceptance, other contractors' deviations, or an inspector's
unauthorized indulgence do not (*Moore Electric*; *Hoboken Shipyards*).

## Acceptance (RFO FAR 46.5)
- The CO's (or assigned office's) act assuming ownership/approving
  services; watch delegation — a COTR without acceptance authority
  can't accept (*Design & Production*). Usually via DD 250.
- **Implied acceptance**: final payment, unreasonable delay in
  rejecting (*Cudahy* — two months for eggs), or Government use/
  alteration inconsistent with contractor ownership (*Ateron*).
- Acceptance passes title, triggers payment, and is **conclusive**
  except: **latent defects** (unknown + existing at acceptance + not
  discoverable by reasonable inspection — *Munson Hammerhead*;
  *Wickham* spliced cables), **fraud** (intent + material
  misrepresentation + reliance — counterfeit UL labels, *D&H*),
  **gross mistake amounting to fraud** (*Bender GmbH* — wanton
  disregard), or contract terms (warranties).
- Revocation revives inspection/default/disputes rights (52.246-2(l))
  — but sit on it and lose it (*Perkin-Elmer* — six years too long).
- **Risk of loss** stays on the contractor until acceptance (97%-
  complete building lost to hurricane = contractor's loss, *DeRalco*);
  f.o.b. destination keeps transit risk on the contractor even after
  early acceptance; construction use-and-possession shifts risk for
  damage from Government possession (52.236-11).

## Warranties (RFO FAR 46.7)
- Optional, cost-vs-benefit call; they **extend** the finality window
  for covered defects and survive acceptance (*Shelby's Gourmet
  Foods*).
- Government's proof burden on a warranty claim: defect at completion;
  warranted defect was the **most probable cause** (*R.B. Hazard*);
  within scope; within period; no Government contribution/misuse;
  specific **notice** to the contractor (*Land O'Frost*); and repair
  cost. Improper Government maintenance/operation voids coverage.
- Remedies: order repair/replace or keep at reduced price; on
  contractor failure — fix in-house/by contract and charge it, or take
  an equitable adjustment (not below scrap value). Remedies stated in
  the clause are exclusive (*Kordick*) but cumulative with inspection
  remedies (*Charles G. Williams*).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The Government's leverage evaporates at acceptance — inspect before,
  not after; destructive testing post-acceptance won't save a claim the
  pre-acceptance inspection could have caught (*Ahern*).
- Deduction schedules on service contracts are powerful but may cost
  you the default termination for those same months — decide which
  remedy you want.
- For contractors: document every inspection standard change in
  writing; overzealous inspection is one of the most winnable claim
  theories.
- Warranty claims die on notice defects — tell the contractor exactly
  what failed and which warranty covers it, promptly.
