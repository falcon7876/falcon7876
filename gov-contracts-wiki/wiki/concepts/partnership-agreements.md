# Partnership Agreements (Cooperative Agreements, CRADAs, PIAs, TIAs, EPAs, IGSAs)

**One-line:** When the principal purpose is to support or stimulate a
public purpose — not to buy something for the Government — the right
instrument isn't a procurement contract but one of the assistance and
technology-transfer vehicles, each with its own statute, its own
approval authority, and its own IP quirks.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 34 (whole
chapter).*

## The instrument-selection triangle
Under the Federal Grant and Cooperative Agreement Act (now 31 U.S.C.
§§ 6301-6308), the choice is principal-purpose-driven:
- **Procurement contract** — acquiring property/services for the
  Government's **direct benefit or use** (or whenever fee/profit is
  paid).
- **Grant** — transferring a thing of value for a **public purpose of
  support or stimulation**, with **minimal** federal involvement.
- **Cooperative agreement** (31 U.S.C. § 6305) — same assistance
  purpose as a grant, but with **substantial involvement** expected
  between the agency and the recipient. Document the rationale — and
  never pick a cooperative agreement merely to get tighter controls
  than a grant would allow.

## Cooperative agreements
- Not FAR contracts (FAR 2.101); most of the FAR/DFARS doesn't apply
  (32 C.F.R. § 21.325). Governed by OMB rules (2 C.F.R. 200), DoD's
  2 C.F.R. 1104, and the **DoDGARs** (32 C.F.R. subtit. A, ch. I,
  subch. C).
- DoD authorities include 10 U.S.C. § 2391 (base-closure impact
  mitigation with state/local governments), § 4001 (R&D projects),
  § 4954 (procurement technical assistance — usually capped at 75%
  of the entity's cost).
- Excluded by definition: cash assistance to individuals, subsidies,
  loans, loan guarantees, insurance — and CRADAs (separately
  authorized under 15 U.S.C. § 3710a).
- Award discipline still exists: **merit-based competitive
  procedures** where possible, recipient qualification (management
  capability, finances, record of integrity), disputes provisions,
  reporting in the Defense Assistance Award System. Accounting
  treatment matches grants.

## Educational Partnership Agreements (10 U.S.C. § 2194)
- Defense-laboratory directors partner with educational institutions
  (any level) to promote STEM study. Menu: loan or transfer (surplus)
  lab equipment, lab personnel teaching, faculty sabbaticals, paid
  student internships, academic credit for lab work, and separate
  contracts/cooperative agreements/grants to fund the activities.
- No standard format — writing plus the basics (authority, purpose,
  mutual benefit, actions, dates, POCs, and IP/security terms as
  needed); any duration the parties agree.

## Technology transfer & transition (T3)
Federal policy (Stevenson-Wydler + FTTA, 15 U.S.C. § 3710) obliges
labs to push federally funded technology out to states and the private
sector. Every federal lab must staff an **ORTA** (Office of Research
and Technology Applications) to assess and market lab technology —
without substantially competing with private-sector services.

### CRADAs (15 U.S.C. § 3710a)
- Federal lab commits personnel, facilities, equipment, IP —
  **anything but funds** — while the collaborator may contribute
  everything **including funds**. Approval authority is the **lab
  director**, not a CO. GOGO and GOCO labs only.
- Process: concept → technical discussions → **Joint Work Statement**
  (agency approves/modifies/disapproves within 30 days; no CRADA
  before JWS approval) → legal/financial reviews → perform → final
  report identifying inventions.
- IP: collaborator may retain ownership of resulting inventions and
  can get patent licenses/options (even pre-negotiated exclusive
  field-of-use licenses), but the Government **always retains a
  nonexclusive, irrevocable, paid-up license** to practice the
  invention worldwide. Statutory preferences: small businesses, and
  U.S.-located businesses agreeing to substantially-U.S. manufacture.
- No duplicating research already funded under existing Government
  programs. SBIR/STTR collaborators trigger 15 U.S.C. § 638(ee)
  protections.

### Partnership Intermediary Agreements (15 U.S.C. § 3715; 10 U.S.C. § 4124)
- Lab (a designated **Center**/STRL) hires an intermediary — a state/
  local agency or a state-chartered/funded nonprofit — via contract,
  MOU, or OT to broker cooperation with small businesses and
  universities (spin-off and spin-on). A warranted CO **may but need
  not** be involved; technology-transfer funds pay the federal costs.
- Limits: can't convert Center work from Government personnel to
  contractor performance; federal-laboratory parties only. Examples:
  MilTech (Montana State), BRICC (VT-ARC for AFOSR).

### Fee-for-service ("work for others")
- **10 U.S.C. § 4175** — commercial T&E at Major Ranges (direct-cost
  reimbursement mandatory, indirect optional).
- **10 U.S.C. § 4892** — sell/rent/lend equipment, information, and
  services at labs (DoDI 5535.11: recoup **total** cost absent
  compelling reason; no undue competition with the private sector;
  Government typically gets no IP rights in the test data).
- **10 U.S.C. § 2474** — public-private partnerships using excess
  depot/arsenal capacity; acts as a relief valve on the § 2466
  **50/50 rule** capping contractor depot maintenance.

### Technology Investment Agreements (32 C.F.R. Part 37)
- Dual-natured assistance instruments straddling 10 U.S.C. § 4001
  and § 4021: a TIA whose patent terms follow **Bayh-Dole** is a
  cooperative agreement; one with **less restrictive** patent terms
  is awarded as an assistance (research-OT) transaction — so the AO's
  appointment must expressly authorize TIAs.
- **No fee or profit** to awardee or subawardees; typically
  cost-shared (cash or in-kind — but NOT IP or prior research
  costs); "fixed-support" or "expenditure-based" structures; award
  competitively as policy. Remember *The Boeing Co.* (ASBCA
  No. 60373): TIA funding didn't count as "government contract"
  funding under DFARS 7014 — see [Other Transaction
  Authority](other-transaction-authority.md).

## Intergovernmental Support Agreements (10 U.S.C. § 2679)
- Installation ↔ state/local/tribal government partnerships for
  **installation-support services** (trash, grounds, utilities O&M,
  snow removal, pest/animal control — NOT security guard or
  firefighting), awardable **sole-source** if: the Secretary finds
  best-interest (mission effectiveness or economies), the government
  partner **already provides the service for its own use**, and any
  underlying private contract is **competitively awarded** by
  whichever party lets it.
- Max **10 years** (renewals allowed per service policy); can't be
  used to dodge A-76 analysis (see [Competitive Sourcing &
  Privatization](competitive-sourcing-privatization.md)); O&M funds
  pay; FAR doesn't govern the IGSA itself but does govern resulting
  procurements. Service approval ladders scale with annual value
  (e.g., Army: IMCOM ≤$1M/yr → DASA(IE&E) >$1M → ASA(IE&E) >$3M,
  routed through OACSIM).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- Instrument choice is a legal conclusion, not a preference: buying
  for the Government's direct benefit (or paying fee/profit) forces a
  procurement contract; assistance purposes forbid one.
- CRADA money flows one way only — the lab can take the
  collaborator's funds but can never send funds out. If the
  Government needs to fund the partner, you're in
  grant/cooperative-agreement/OT territory.
- The Government's retained paid-up license under a CRADA is
  non-negotiable; everything else about the IP split is.
- A TIA's legal character (cooperative agreement vs. OT) is decided
  by its patent clause — check the AO's appointment covers both.
- IGSAs are sole-source-friendly at the government-to-government
  layer, but the competition requirement reattaches the moment a
  private contractor performs the underlying work.
