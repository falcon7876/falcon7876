# Contingency & Deployment Contracting (and Contractor Personnel)

**One-line:** There is no "deployment exception" to contract or fiscal
law — contingency operations just raise the thresholds, add theater
machinery (boards, FOOs, CAP contracts), and bring a battlefield
population of contractor personnel whose status, support, and
discipline are governed by the contract, the SOFA, and MEJA/UCMJ.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook chs. 29-30 (whole
chapters).*

## Governing framework (ch. 29)
- The FAR/DFARS apply in full downrange; the load-bearing parts are
  Part 6 (competition), Part 12/13 (simplified — **~95% of contingency
  actions**), Part 18 (emergency flexibilities), Part 25 (foreign
  acquisition; BAA doesn't apply to items used overseas), and Part 50
  (extraordinary actions).
- **Command authority ≠ contract authority.** Commanders direct
  operations (10 U.S.C. § 164) but cannot obligate funds; contracting
  authority flows SecDef → HCA → SCO/PARC → warranted **contracting
  officer** (SF 1402). A commander "directing" a contractor creates an
  unauthorized commitment.
- Elevated **contingency thresholds**: MPT $15K → $25K (inside U.S.)/
  $40K (outside); SAT $350K → $1M/$2M ($650K humanitarian-
  peacekeeping); commercial SAP $9M → $15M.
- Temporary operation-specific authorities ride appropriations/NDAAs
  (e.g., CERP, Afghan First); Pub. L. 85-804 extraordinary authority
  exists during declared national emergencies but can't waive
  competition and is rarely used.

## Theater organization & people
- **Personnel chain**: HCA → SCO (formerly PARC) → COs (PCO/ACO/TCO)
  → **CORs** (eyes and ears, no contract authority; designate and
  train before award) → **Field Ordering Officers** (unit members
  appointed by a CO: SF 44s to the MPT, BPA calls, delivery orders per
  their appointment letter) paired with **paying agents** (finance-
  appointed, hold and disburse cash; a FOO may not be a paying agent).
- **Three sources of contracted support** (JP 4-10):
  1. **Theater support contracts** — awarded in the operational area
     under the theater HCA (the classic "contingency contract"; mostly
     local-national workforce).
  2. **Systems support contracts** — PMO-awarded weapon-system
     support; only the issuing activity can modify them; workforce
     mostly U.S.-citizen CAAF.
  3. **External support contracts** — CONUS-awarded logistics
     programs: **LOGCAP** (Army), AFCAP, Navy GCCC/GCSC, DLA fuel and
     supply. Long-term, cost-plus-award-fee ID/IQs; more expensive
     than theater contracts, so transition off them ASAP. Only
     warranted COs (not commanders) change LOGCAP, including Letters
     of Technical Direction.
- Theater contracting organization options: each Service supports its
  own forces, a **lead Service**, or a **joint theater support
  contracting command** (HCA authority borrowed from a Service).
- **Requirements pipeline**: requiring activity builds a "procurement-
  ready" package (requirement + market research + IGE + PWS + certified
  PR&C funding) → review boards — **CLPSB** (GCC-level policy),
  **JRRB** (validates/prioritizes requirements; JA sits as non-voting
  advisor), **JCSB** (matches requirements to contract vehicles) →
  contracting activity. Legal review covers funding, method, HN/SOFA
  issues, and judgment calls like armed PSC use.
- Planning artifacts: **CSIP** (Annex W — how contracted support is
  acquired/integrated) and **CMP** (Government obligations TO
  contractor personnel: theater entry, accountability, force
  protection, CAAF status calls made early with the SJA).

## Contracting methods downrange (ch. 29)
- CICA applies — no automatic deployment exception. The urgency
  exception (6.103-2) is easier to justify operationally (sandbags for
  OIF, *Total Industrial*) but **never** for self-inflicted urgency
  (*WorldWide Language* — sole-source linguists sustained where the
  agency sat on the requirement).
- Sealed bidding is rare in theater (discussions usually needed);
  negotiations and simplified procedures dominate. Local-source-only
  overseas actions are exempt from GPE publication (FAR 5.302(b)).
- Prefer existing vehicles: theater IDIQs/BPAs (fair-opportunity task
  orders), the military supply system, **ACSAs/host-nation support**
  (10 U.S.C. §§ 2341-50 — LSSS transfers with allies), and **Economy
  Act** orders (see [Interagency Transactions](interagency-transactions.md)).
  Foreign real estate leasing via 10 U.S.C. § 2675 (USACE CREST teams).
- **Policing the battlefield**: ratification (FAR 1.405) for
  unauthorized commitments; Part 50 formalization of informal
  commitments (request within 6 months); quantum meruit for benefits
  received without contract; CDA implied-in-fact claims — same toolkit
  as [Authority to Contract](authority-to-contract.md).
- **Vendor threat mitigation** ("contracting with the enemy," FY24
  NDAA § 823): HCAs may restrict award to, terminate, or void contracts
  with entities supporting forces hostile to the U.S. — now **no
  dollar threshold, all COCOMs, all operations outside the U.S.**;
  battlefield intelligence may ground the decision without disclosure;
  companion records-access clauses (DFARS 252.225-7993/-7994).

## Contractor personnel (ch. 30)
- **The contract is the charter**: it fixes status, support, and
  obligations. Every deployed contractor carries a **Letter of
  Authorization** (issued through **SPOT**, the mandatory tracking
  database) listing entitlements.
- **CAAF** (contractors authorized to accompany the force): embedded
  with units, Government-furnished support per the contract, Geneva
  Conventions ID (DD 489) → **POW status if captured**; neither
  combatants nor noncombatants — direct participation in hostilities
  risks unprivileged-belligerent treatment. Non-CAAF (theater local
  nationals, contractors outside the AOR) get less support and
  different clauses (52.225-19; DFARS 252.225-7995 deviation in
  CENTCOM).
- **Status under local law** rides the SOFA (contractor employees are
  generally NOT "civilian component" under the NATO SOFA — separate
  arrangements needed); no SOFA = local criminal/tax jurisdiction and
  possible entry problems. SOFA trumps the contract on conflict.
- **Support menu** (per contract/LOA): deployment processing at CRC,
  theater reception (JRC), IPE/body armor, CAC, emergency
  **resuscitative medical care only** (reimbursable; no primary or
  long-term care), MWR/exchange and military post for U.S.-citizen
  CAAF, evacuation assistance, personnel recovery, mortuary affairs.
  No legal assistance for contractors.
- **Arming & security**: COCOM approves individual self-defense
  arming case-by-case through the CO (screen Lautenberg); **private
  security contractors** (DoDI 3020.50) perform defensive functions
  only — security is never an inherently governmental combat role, and
  arming approvals + rules for the use of force run through the COCOM.
- **Command & discipline**: commanders control installations (badge/
  access revocation, force-protection orders) but do NOT supervise
  contractor personnel; only the CO directs the contractor (including
  removal of employees — beware wrongful-termination exposure).
  Criminal accountability: **MEJA** (federal jurisdiction over DoD
  contractors/employees/dependents overseas for felonies; *Brehm*) and
  **UCMJ Art. 2(a)(10)** (extended to contingency operations by the
  FY07 NDAA; DOJ gets right of first refusal; *United States v. Ali*
  upheld a court-martial of a contractor) — constitutionality still
  debated.
- Benefits: Defense Base Act workers'-comp insurance mandatory on
  overseas contracts (see [Labor Standards](labor-standards.md)); War
  Hazards Compensation Act covers war-risk injury regardless of scope
  of employment; contractor service earns **no veterans' benefits**;
  no "desertion" offense — plan for contractor non-performance in a
  crisis (essential-services continuity clauses, DoDI 3020.41).
- **Combating trafficking in persons** (FAR 22.17; 52.222-50): no
  passport confiscation, native-language employment contracts,
  living-space minimums (CENTCOM E1-equivalent standard) — a
  compliance area with real enforcement history.

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The recurring battlefield failure is commanders (or CORs) "tasking"
  contractors — every AAR says the same thing: route direction through
  the CO or budget for a ratification file.
- Check the SOFA before promising anything to a contractor about
  taxes, weapons, or criminal exposure; the contract can't override it.
- CAAF status decisions are made at contract formation — retrofitting
  support entitlements in theater is painful; get the DFARS clause and
  LOA right up front.
- The vendor-threat-mitigation authority is now global and
  threshold-free; build the records-access clauses into overseas
  contracts and know the HCA's void/terminate powers.
- Urgency J&As write themselves in a contingency — but GAO still
  sustains protests where the "urgency" was the Government's own delay.
