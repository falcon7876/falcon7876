# Construction Contracting

**One-line:** Construction runs on its own FAR Part 36 machinery —
design-bid-build vs. design-build delivery, Brooks Act
qualifications-based A-E selection, Miller Act bonds, and a cluster of
clauses (differing site conditions, suspension, LDs) that generate most
of the claims.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 28 (whole
chapter).*

## Definitions & fiscal frame
- "Construction" (RFO FAR 2.101) = construction/alteration/repair of
  **real property** (dredging, excavating, painting count; work on
  vessels/aircraft/personal property doesn't). "Military construction"
  and "military construction project" per 10 U.S.C. § 2801 (complete
  and usable facility).
- **Funding tiers** (FY24 NDAA): ≤$4M O&M; $4-9M UMMC; >$9M MILCON.
  For fiscal purposes **repair/maintenance isn't construction** — O&M
  funds regardless of cost. Contract formation follows FAR Part 36
  regardless of funding color (details in the TJAGLCS Fiscal Law
  Deskbook; see [Funding & Fund
  Limitations](funding-and-fund-limitations.md)).
- **IGE required** above the SAT for contracts and mods (marked CUI,
  not shared with offerors).

## Delivery methods
- **Sealed bidding** when the *Racal*-style conditions hold; negotiate
  overseas and always for A-E services.
- **Design-bid-build**: two contracts — A-E designs, builder builds to
  the Government's design (Government owns design risk — see *Spearin*
  line under specs below).
- **Two-phase design-build** (10 U.S.C. § 3241): default when ≥3
  offers expected and offerors must do substantial design work to
  price. Phase one — qualifications/technical, **no price**, shortlist
  (max 5 absent findings; DoD >$5.5M caps at 5); phase two — full
  technical + price proposals under Part 15.
- **FY26 NDAA alternatives**: **Progressive Design-Build** (single-
  phase qualifications selection, collaborate on scope/design, then
  negotiate a guaranteed maximum price — off-ramps if negotiation
  fails); **Accelerated Design-Build** (select the DB contractor before
  project authorization/appropriation; T4C liability capped at design
  costs); **OTAs for facility repair/construction** (new § 2802
  authority, few stated limits — watch implementing guidance);
  **Design-Build-to-Budget** (disclose the budget ceiling; compete on
  value within it).
- **Construction as a commercial service**: the RFO definition now
  includes construction sold competitively at market prices — but Part
  12 strips the standard construction clauses (progress payments,
  suspension, changes, default), while construction-unique statutes
  (labor, bonds) still apply. Use with care; the old OFPP "rarely if
  ever" caution still frames the analysis.
- Contract types: **FFP is the norm** (lump-sum preferred over unit
  pricing unless quantities are uncertain); FP/EPA when customary;
  **CPFF banned** for MILCON-funded work >$25K in CONUS absent
  secretarial approval; ID/IQ construction is lawful (*Tyler
  Construction*, Fed. Cir.); **Job Order Contracts** (AFARS 5117.90) —
  IDIQ with a priced task book × contractor coefficient for
  repair/maintenance/minor construction (not for recurring facility
  services, A-E, or design-build).

## Solicitation & award
- Presolicitation notice >$20K; SF 1442 above the SAT; include
  **statutory cost limitations** in separate schedules — offers over
  the limit (or under it only via material unbalancing) are rejected
  (*Tadlock*). A-E fee caps: 6% of estimated construction cost
  (design work; 10% for military departments per DFARS PGI).
- **Site investigation clause** (52.236-3): bidders are charged with
  everything a reasonable site visit would reveal (*H.B. Mac*) —
  though no duty to hire geologists or verify Government drawings
  (*Michael-Mark*). Post-bid site changes are the Government's cost
  (*Valley Construction*).
- **Bid guarantees** (required whenever performance/payment bonds
  will be; ≥20% of price, max $3M): a defective or facially
  unenforceable guarantee makes a sealed bid **nonresponsive**
  (*Leeward*), though nine waiver situations exist; in negotiated
  buys it's a discussable deficiency. Individual sureties are a
  responsibility matter; a bidder can't be its own surety.
- Construction responsiveness traps: missing SF 1442, exceeding a
  statutory cost cap, short acceptance period, unacknowledged material
  (incl. Davis-Bacon wage) amendments, equivocating on permits,
  material unbalancing.
- **Overseas MILCON preference**: Pacific outlying areas/Kwajalein/
  Arabian Gulf projects >$1M go to U.S. firms unless a foreign offer
  beats the U.S. offer by >20% (evaluation factor per DFARS
  252.236-7010); A-E >$500K in Japan/NATO/Gulf: U.S. firms or
  U.S.-host-nation JVs only.
- **A-E selection (Brooks Act, 40 U.S.C. § 1101)**:
  qualifications-based — evaluation board reviews SF 330-type
  qualifications, holds discussions with ≥3 firms, recommends ≥3 in
  order of preference; selection authority picks; CO then negotiates
  price with the #1 firm only, terminating and moving down the list if
  no fair-and-reasonable agreement. Price competition never ranks the
  firms. Performance evaluations: construction ≥$900K, A-E ≥$45K, all
  default-terminated contracts.

## Administration — the claims engine
- **Bonds (Miller Act, 40 U.S.C. §§ 3131-34)**: >$150K — performance
  bond (100% of price) + payment bond (100%, never less than the
  performance bond) before work starts; $35-150K — two alternative
  payment protections (letters of credit favored). Waivable overseas
  where impracticable. **No bonds = material breach = T4D** (*Airport
  Industrial Park*). Withholding: don't withhold mid-performance for
  unpaid subs (setoff rights aside, *Johnson v. All-State*), but MUST
  withhold final payment on surety notice.
- **Differing site conditions** (52.236-2):
  - **Type I** — conditions differ materially from what the contract
    **indicated**: contract indication + reasonable reliance +
    materially different latent condition + costs solely attributable.
    No representations = no recovery.
  - **Type II** — unknown, **unusual** physical conditions differing
    from those ordinarily encountered (buried dumps, hazmat).
  - Limits: conditions must exist at award (weather isn't a DSC,
    *Arundel*); no recovery for what a reasonable site investigation
    would find; prompt written notice (relaxed absent prejudice,
    *Parker Excavating*); **no DSC clause, no claim** (*Marine
    Industries NW*); final payment bars unreserved claims.
- **Suspension of work** (52.242-14): compensable when the delay is
  unreasonable, Government-caused, not contractor-fault, and costly —
  **no profit**; constructive suspensions from late notice-to-proceed
  or failure to give direction; early-finish delay damages possible
  (*Oneida*); concurrent intertwined delay = nobody recovers
  (*Wilner*); 20-day pre-notice cost limit (prejudice-tested,
  *George Sollitt*).
- **Permits & responsibilities** (52.236-7): contractor gets permits
  and complies with laws — **including post-award ones** — at no extra
  cost (*Shirley Construction*); bears risk of loss to work in
  progress until acceptance; liable for third-party injuries.
- **Specs & drawings** (52.236-21): omitted details customarily
  performed must still be built; contractor is charged with errors a
  reasonable drawing review would catch (*M.A. Mortenson*);
  **specifications trump drawings** in conflicts (*Hensel Phelps*);
  the Government cannot disclaim liability for defective **design**
  specs (*White v. Edsall* — no duty to ferret out hidden errors).
- **Liquidated damages** (52.211-12): enforceable if intended, damages
  hard to estimate at award, and rate a reasonable forecast (*K-Con*);
  actual damages unnecessary; **no LDs after substantial completion**
  (*Hill Construction*) or where the Government shares delay fault
  (*H.G. Reynolds*); excusable delay remits them (contractor's burden,
  *Sauer*).
- **Use/possession prior to completion** (52.236-11): beneficial
  occupancy ≠ acceptance (punch list survives); Government pays for
  damage it causes and for delays its possession creates.

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The DSC clause is the contractor's only shield against subsurface
  risk — leasing or nonstandard instruments that omit it shift the
  entire risk to the contractor.
- Bid-bond defects are unforgivable in sealed bidding; check the penal
  sum, the surety's Circular 570 listing, and enforceability before
  opening day.
- For the Government: reasonable completion dates keep LD clauses
  enforceable and bids un-padded; document the LD rate math at
  solicitation time.
- A-E is qualifications-first by statute — evaluating price to rank
  firms is a protestable Brooks Act violation.
- The 2026 alternative-delivery authorities (PDB/ADB/OTA/DBtB) are
  powerful but new — verify implementing DFARS/service guidance
  before committing a project.
