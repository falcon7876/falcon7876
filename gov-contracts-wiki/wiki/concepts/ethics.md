# Ethics in Government Contracting

**One-line:** Three overlapping regimes police the people in
procurement: 18 U.S.C. § 208 (financial conflicts while serving), the
Procurement Integrity Act (procurement info + job-hunting +
$10M revolving door), and 18 U.S.C. § 207 (side-switching after
leaving).

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 23 (whole
chapter).*

## Financial conflicts of interest (18 U.S.C. § 208)
- Prohibition: no **personal and substantial** participation in a
  **particular matter** with a **direct and predictable effect** on the
  employee's financial interests. Applies to officers and civilians
  directly; enlisted members via the Joint Ethics Regulation (UCMJ-
  punishable); screen reservists before activation (JER 5-408).
- **Imputed interests**: spouse, minor child, general partner, any
  organization the employee serves (officer/director/trustee/employee),
  and **anyone the employee is negotiating with for employment**.
- **Negotiating/seeking employment is the tripwire**: interpreted
  broadly (*Schaltenbrand*) — a lunch about future prospects counts.
  OGE "seeking employment" is broader still: any non-rejection response
  to a recruiter triggers it; it ends only on affirmative rejection or
  two months of silence after an unsolicited résumé. STOCK Act: OGE 278
  filers must report negotiations within 3 business days.
- Remedies: **disqualification** (default; written notice to
  supervisor), **individual waiver** (interest "not so substantial"),
  **divestiture**, or a regulatory exemption — diversified mutual
  funds/ETFs are always fine; sector funds exempt up to **$50K**
  aggregate; de minimis securities exemptions; TSP/state pensions.
- Penalties: up to 1 year (5 if willful) + $50K per violation; the
  contractor business-ethics clause 52.203-13 rides on contracts >$6M/
  120+ days.

## Procurement Integrity Act (41 U.S.C. §§ 2101-07; FAR 3.104)
Three prongs:
1. **Information**: no knowing disclosure OR obtaining of **contractor
   bid or proposal information** (cost/pricing data, indirect rates,
   marked proprietary data) or **source selection information** (bid
   prices, evaluations, competitive range, rankings, SSEB reports)
   before award. Doesn't reach bidder-to-bidder leaks (*GEO Group*) —
   but the CO can still disqualify for integrity taint short of a
   violation (*Compliance Corp.* — industrial espionage; *NKF
   Engineering* — appearance of impropriety).
2. **Employment contacts**: an official participating personally and
   substantially in a >SAT acquisition who is contacted by (or
   contacts) a bidder about employment must **report in writing**
   (supervisor + ethics official) and either promptly reject or
   disqualify (notice to HCA, CO, SSA, supervisor). "Personal and
   substantial" = drafting the SOW, preparing the solicitation,
   evaluating, negotiating, approving award — not clerical or broad-
   policy work.
3. **One-year compensation ban**: a former official may not accept
   compensation from a contractor within one year if, on a contract
   **>$10M** to that contractor, they served as PCO/ACO, SSA, SSEB
   member, evaluation-team chief, or PM/DPM — or personally made a
   >$10M decision (award, rates, payment, claim settlement). Clock
   starts at selection/award/last day in position/decision date as
   applicable. Working for a **different division or affiliate** that
   doesn't make the same products is allowed. Advisory opinions
   available (FAR 3.104-6).
- Penalties: 5 years criminal (for value/competitive advantage); civil
  $50K individual / $500K organization + 2× compensation; procurement
  cancellation, rescission, S&D, profit-recapture clauses (52.203-8,
  -10).
- **Protest trap**: a PIA-based protest requires the protester to have
  reported the violation to the agency within **14 days** of discovery
  (41 U.S.C. § 2106).

## Post-Government employment (18 U.S.C. § 207)
Restricts *how*, not *whether*, a former employee works:
- **Permanent ban** (§ 207(a)(1)): never represent anyone back to the
  Government on a particular matter (with specific parties) in which
  you participated personally and substantially.
- **Two-year ban** (§ 207(a)(2)): same, for matters that were pending
  under your **official responsibility** in your final year.
- **One-year bans**: § 207(c) "cooling off" for senior employees
  (GO/FO, SES) — no representational contacts with the former agency
  (DoD is split into components, so a Navy admiral may contact the
  Army); § 207(b) — no aid/advice (even behind-the-scenes) on ongoing
  trade/treaty negotiations based on nonpublic information.
- Behind-the-scenes work is generally allowed under § 207(a) (not
  (b)/(f)); enlisted members are outside § 207 entirely. Terminal-leave
  officers: no representing anyone before the Government (18 U.S.C.
  § 205) and no exercising state/local sovereign authority (10 U.S.C.
  § 973).
- **DoD § 847 overlay** (DFARS 203.171-3; 252.203-7000): "covered DoD
  officials" (E.S./SES/GO-FO or key acquisition roles on >$10M
  actions) need a **written ethics opinion** before a DoD contractor
  may pay them within 2 years of leaving — the burden is on the
  contractor, on pain of rescission or S&D.

## Contractor-side and process rules
- **Contractor personal conflicts of interest** (FAR 3.11; 52.203-16):
  contractors performing functions "closely associated with inherently
  governmental" must screen covered employees for PCIs and stop misuse
  of nonpublic information; CO reviews reported issues.
- **Dealing with industry**: above reproach, impartial, no preferential
  treatment (FAR 3.101-1). Pre-solicitation exchanges are encouraged
  (FAR 15.201) — but release acquisition information identically,
  simultaneously, and only through the CO; never advance information
  about future solicitations.
- Housekeeping: termination OGE 278 within 30 days of leaving; annual
  post-employment certifications (JER 8-400); out-processing ethics
  counseling (JER 9-402); purchase-card safeguards (10 U.S.C. § 2784
  for DoD — cardholders never approve their own buys).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The job-hunt rules bite earliest: not saying "no" to a recruiter's
  email is "seeking employment," and one lunch is "negotiating" —
  report and disqualify before the next meeting on that program.
- The PIA one-year ban is role- and contract-specific; § 207 bans are
  matter-specific; DoD § 847 adds a 2-year opinion-letter gate. Run all
  three checklists for every departing acquisition official.
- Protest counsel: calendar the 14-day PIA report — miss it and GAO
  won't hear the violation at all.
- The CO can disqualify an offeror on appearance alone with hard facts
  — integrity of the process outranks proof of a statutory violation.
