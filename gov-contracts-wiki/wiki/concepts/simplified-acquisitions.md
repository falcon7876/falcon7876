# Simplified Acquisitions & the Federal Supply Schedule

**One-line:** Below the thresholds, the FAR trades ceremony for speed —
RFQs where the *Government's order* is the offer, purchase cards, BPAs
that aren't contracts, and FSS ordering that counts as full and open
competition by statute.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 10 (whole
chapter).*

## The thresholds (FAR 2.101; memorize the normal column)
| Purchase type | Normal | Contingency/CNBCR etc., inside U.S. | Outside U.S. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro-purchase (MPT) | **$15,000** | $25,000 | $40,000 |
| Simplified acquisition (SAT) | **$350,000** | $1,000,000 | $2,000,000 |
| Commercial products/services (via SAP) | **$9,000,000** | $15,000,000 | $15,000,000 |

Construction MPT is $2,000; Service Contract Labor Standards MPT is
$2,500. Humanitarian/peacekeeping ops outside the U.S.: SAT $650K.
"In support of" determinations sit with the agency head (ASA(ALT) for
Army, with AFARS App. GG delegations).

## Ground rules (RFO FAR Part 13)
- Use SAP "to the maximum extent practicable" at or below the SAT —
  *unless* the need is met by a required source (RFO FAR Part 8: FPI,
  AbilityOne, FSS), an existing ID/IQ, or another established contract.
- RFO FAR 13 procedures apply only where **no commercial
  product/service** satisfies the need (commercial buys ride RFO FAR
  Part 12 — see [Commercial Products & Services](commercial-products-services.md)).
- **No splitting** requirements to stay under thresholds (RFO FAR
  13.001(c)) — but indeterminate, as-needed emergency buys can survive
  (*Petchem*; *Mas-Hamilton*).
- **Small-business set-aside is automatic** between the MPT and SAT
  (RFO FAR 13.102), subject to the Rule of Two and the
  outside-the-U.S./required-source exceptions.

## Competition: "maximum extent practicable," not full and open
- CICA exempts SAP from full and open competition (10 U.S.C. § 3205);
  the working standard is: post a pre-solicitation on SAM.gov, **solicit
  at least three sources**, and rotate in two new ones when practicable.
- GPE posting = constructive notice, even to incumbents (*PR Newswire*;
  *Optelec*) — but **deliberately excluding** a source that expressed
  interest, without a reasonable basis, loses protests (*Gateway Cable*
  — vendor called the CO 19 times; *Bosco Contracting*; *Solutions
  Lucid Group*).
- Sole source under the SAT: CO documents that only one source is
  reasonably available (urgency, exclusive license, brand name) —
  no formal J&A until over the SAT. Even then, give would-be quoters a
  *real* chance to respond to the sole-source notice (*Jack Faucett* —
  one day is not reasonable).
- Micro-purchases: **no competition required** if the price is
  reasonable; distribute equitably among suppliers (rotation lists are
  fine, *Grimm's Orthopedic*).

## RFQ mechanics — the offer/acceptance flip
- A **quotation is not an offer**. The Government's purchase order is
  the offer; the vendor accepts by written notice, furnishing the
  supplies, or **substantial performance** (*Giancola v. GSA* — vendor
  who received and began reviewing course materials had accepted; the
  Government then owed a T4C settlement, not a free cancellation).
- Before acceptance the Government may withdraw/cancel the order
  freely; after acceptance it must **terminate**, with a settlement
  (RFO FAR 13.302). A vendor's response changing terms is a
  counteroffer (*C&M Machine*).
- Cancelling an RFQ needs only a reasonable basis (needs changed,
  defective criteria, item added to a required-source list).
- Evaluations: CO has broad discretion; Parts 14/15 procedures are
  optional — but stated criteria still bind (*Low & Assocs.*), unequal
  or meaningless discussions still sustain protests (*Tipton Textile*;
  *Academy Leadership*), and the award decision must be **documented**
  (*Universal Building Maintenance*; *Resource Dimensions* — no record,
  agency loses).
- Innovation is encouraged (reverse auctions OK, *MTB Group*; but DFARS
  bars them for PPE/aviation critical safety items).

## Purchasing vehicles
- **Purchase orders** (SF 1449 / DD 1155); **unpriced purchase orders**
  when pricing must follow (repairs needing disassembly) — include a
  monetary ceiling and the withhold-performance notice.
- **SF 44**: pocket order form for on-the-spot, over-the-counter buys
  (≤ MPT; contingency/intel uses in DoD).
- **BPAs**: "charge accounts" with qualified sources. **A BPA is not a
  contract** — no CDA jurisdiction (*Zhengxing*, 71 Fed. Cl. 732); the
  contract forms order-by-order. Each order still needs competition and
  set-aside compliance (*Envirosolve*); individual purchases capped at
  the SAT; review annually.
- **Imprest funds**: nearly dead in DoD (contingency/classified only).
- **GPC (purchase card)**: mandatory DoD method at/below the MPT.
  Required sources still apply. Never issue to contractors. Classic
  audit findings: split purchases, weak approving-official ratios
  (keep ≤5-7 cardholders each), missing documentation.

## Federal Supply Schedule (RFO FAR 8.4 / GSAM 538)
- FSS = GSA-run long-term multiple-award IDIQs for commercial items;
  use of FSS procedures **satisfies CICA** by statute (41 U.S.C.
  § 152(3)(B)) when open to all responsible sources and the order gets
  the lowest overall cost alternative.
- **Ordering tiers**: ≤MPT — any schedule holder. MPT-SAT — survey/
  solicit **three** contractors (GSA Advantage, catalogs, eBuy, or
  direct RFQ). >SAT — RFQ to enough holders to reasonably get three
  quotes, or eBuy; document shortfalls.
- **DoD extra step** (RFO DFARS 208.404/217.7; AFARS 5117.770): for
  FSS actions over the SAT, an O-6/GS-15 requiring-activity official
  must approve best-interest, scope, and proper-funds determinations,
  with contracting-office concurrence.
- **Price**: GSA has already found schedule prices fair and reasonable
  — but a DoD class deviation still requires an order-level
  determination. Price must be **meaningfully considered**: GAO killed
  an HTRRP methodology on FSS (*Noble Supply*, B-418141). Seek
  discounts — schedule prices are ceilings, not floors.
- **Scope is strict**: every item/labor category ordered must be ON
  the vendor's schedule — no off-schedule buys (*SAIC*; *Symplicity*;
  *American Systems* — labor categories must "match precisely").
  Lawful paths for extras: **OLMs** (T&M/LH CLIN, ≤33.33% of order/BPA
  value, order-level pricing, no profit), Ancillary Supplies & Services
  SINs, open-market items (with their own competition compliance), or
  **CTAs** (co-prime teaming — every member has privity and can
  protest, *Sirius Federal*).
- **Options/duration**: order options can't run past the schedule's
  ordering period unless the schedule has 52.216-22 (then up to 60
  months past expiration) (*AllWorld Language*). FSS contract must be
  alive at order time (*DRS Precision Echo*).
- **Protests**: FASA's task-order protest bar does NOT apply to FSS
  orders/BPAs (*Severn*; *Analytic Strategies*) — GAO hears them
  regardless of value. Ordering activity defends.
- **Small business**: Part 19 set-asides are discretionary on FSS
  (*American Relocation Connections*) — except the VA, where
  *Kingdomware* (136 S. Ct. 1969) makes the Rule of Two mandatory.
- **Disputes** on FSS orders: ordering CO issues the final decision
  after consulting the schedule CO; appeal to the servicing board or
  COFC.

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The quote/offer flip is the trap for both sides: a vendor who starts
  performing has accepted; a Government that lets performance start can
  no longer cancel for free.
- "Simplified" ≠ undocumented — GAO sustains protests solely for a
  record too thin to review.
- On FSS, scope discipline beats price: an order with one off-schedule
  labor category is protestable no matter how good the deal.
- Splitting purchases to duck a threshold is the #1 purchase-card
  audit finding; watch year-end behavior.
