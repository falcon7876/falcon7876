# Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)

**One-line:** The statutory toolkit (CDA § 7103(h) + ADRA, 5 U.S.C.
§§ 571-584) for resolving protests and contract disputes short of
litigation — always voluntary, and refusals must be explained in
writing by whichever side says no.

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 22 (whole
chapter).*

## Framework
- Government policy: resolve disputes at the CO level (RFO FAR 33.204);
  ADR encouraged whenever the four elements exist (RFO FAR 33.205-8(b)):
  an issue in controversy, both parties willing, agreed procedures, and
  participants with **authority to resolve** the issue.
- Refusals in writing both ways: CO rejecting a contractor's ADR
  request must cite the 5 U.S.C. § 572(b) factors or other specific
  reasons; contractor rejecting a Government request must give written
  reasons (52.233-1(g)).
- Service policies: Air Force "ADR First"; Navy "maximum extent
  practicable" (SECNAVINST 5800.13C); Army ADR Program in OGC — use ADR
  early, cheap, and at the lowest level.

## The continuum (avoidance → adjudication)
1. **Dispute avoidance**: partnering (voluntary teamwork charter, no
   legal rights) and issue-escalation ladders.
2. **Unassisted negotiation.**
3. **Assisted negotiation**: mediation (neutral with no decision power;
   usually *evaluative* in contract disputes — the mediator opines on
   litigation risk); mini-trials (abbreviated presentation to principals
   + neutral, principals then negotiate — Corps of Engineers pedigree).
   Neutrals may be hired without full and open competition (FAR
   6.302-3) and aren't performing inherently governmental functions.
4. **Outcome prediction**: non-binding arbitration; GAO's outcome-
   prediction/litigation-risk conferences in protests (historically
   >80% success resolving the dispute).
5. **Adjudication**: binding arbitration and the ASBCA's **summary
   proceeding with binding decision** (informal trial before a board
   judge; binding, unappealable, non-precedential).

## Binding arbitration — the fine print
- Allowed only after the agency head, consulting the Attorney General,
  issues guidance (5 U.S.C. § 575(c)). In DoD, **only the Navy** has
  (SECNAVINST 5800.15A); Army contract matters can get a binding-
  arbitration-like process only via the ASBCA summary proceeding.
- Agreement must be written, specify a **maximum award**, and be signed
  by someone with settlement authority. Consent can't be a condition of
  award. Award final 30 days after service (extendable 30 more);
  enforceable against the U.S. notwithstanding sovereign immunity
  (5 U.S.C. § 580(c)); judicial review only under FAA § 10
  (partiality, corruption, exceeded powers). DOJ now accepts
  constitutionality if Article III review of constitutional issues and
  fraud/misconduct is preserved (*Tenaska*, 34 Fed. Cl. 434).

## When ADR fits (and doesn't)
- Fits: negotiation impasse, no need for precedent, continuing
  relationship worth preserving, confidentiality matters. ADRA
  confidentiality protects "dispute resolution communications" (FOIA-
  exempt).
- Consider NOT using (5 U.S.C. § 572(b)): precedent needed; significant
  policy questions; consistency across decisions matters; non-parties
  significantly affected; public record needed; continuing agency
  jurisdiction required. These only require *consideration* — ADR can
  proceed despite them. Fraud counterclaims are a practical
  disqualifier.
- Timing: available before or after protest/appeal. GAO offers ADR
  within protests (4 C.F.R. § 21.10(e)); boards suggest ADR at
  docketing; COFC uses RCFC Appendix H (settlement judge, mini-trial).
- The agency's use-or-don't-use decision is not judicially reviewable
  (5 U.S.C. § 581(b)).

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- The written-refusal requirements mean "no" needs a defensible paper
  trail on both sides.
- Verify the participants actually hold settlement authority before
  scheduling — it's an element, not a nicety.
- For Army-side work, don't promise binding arbitration outside the
  ASBCA summary-proceeding route.
