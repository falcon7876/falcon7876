# Government Information Practices (FOIA, Privacy Act, Classification)

**One-line:** Disclosure is the default — FOIA puts the burden of
withholding on the Government — but acquisition work lives inside the
exceptions: classified/CUI rules, the DoD acquisition-information
policy, Exemption 3 (contractor proposals), Exemption 4 (trade
secrets/confidential business info), and Exemption 5 (deliberative
process).

*Compiled from 2026 Acquisition Attorneys Deskbook ch. 35 (whole
chapter).*

## Classified information & the NISP
- E.O. 13526 controls: classification requires (1) an **original
  classification authority**, (2) Government-owned/-controlled
  information, (3) a listed category (military plans, intel, foreign
  relations, WMD, etc.), and (4) an OCA determination that disclosure
  could reasonably damage national security. Levels: Top Secret
  (exceptionally grave) / Secret (serious) / Confidential (damage).
  Derivative classification attaches to derived material; sometimes
  the very **existence** of records is classified (the "**Glomar**"
  neither-confirm-nor-deny response, from *Phillippi v. CIA*).
- Sub-classification controls: **CUI** (FOUO/LIMITED DISTRIBUTION;
  DoDM 5200.01) restricts dissemination without meeting the
  classification standard.
- **NISP** (E.O. 12829) extends protection to contractors: the
  Industrial Security Manual binds contractors via the **DD Form
  441** security agreement and the contract's security clause, and
  COs must issue a **DD Form 254** (Contract Security Classification
  Specification) whenever a contract involves classified access
  (FAR 4.403).

## DoD policy on acquisition-related information (32 C.F.R. § 286h)
Default is public release EXCEPT:
- statutory restrictions; classified information;
- **contractor bid or proposal information** (cost/pricing data,
  rates, processes) — nobody outside the evaluation sees it before
  bid opening/conclusion of negotiations; post-award release only as
  authorized and subject to 52.215-12-type legends;
- **source selection information** (the ten-item list: bid prices,
  evaluation plans and reports, competitive-range determinations,
  rankings, anything giving a competitive advantage) — pre-award
  release only on a component-head public-interest finding;
  post-award the CO may release except reusable evaluation
  materials, protected contractor data, relative merits/scores, and
  FOIA-exempt pre-decisional matter (this mirrors the FAR 3.104 PIA
  regime — see [Ethics](ethics.md));
- PPBS budget documents; negotiating-position documents; drafts and
  working papers.

## Executive privilege / deliberative process
- Protects **pre-decisional, deliberative** intra-government
  material (candid-debate rationale, *Jordan v. DOJ*). Formal
  assertion requires the agency head's personal consideration
  (*United States v. Reynolds*), specific designation of documents,
  and precise confidentiality reasons.
- Limits: material generated **after** the decision isn't covered
  (*Charlesgate* — post-final-decision CO material; *Walsky* — IG
  final report); **purely factual** content isn't covered (*EPA v.
  Mink*) and severable facts must be released; courts can override
  on compelling need, balancing the *Franklin National Bank*
  factors.

## Privacy Act of 1974 (5 U.S.C. § 552a)
- Federal agencies only; protects **individuals** (not corporations —
  *St. Michaels*); applies to **systems of records** retrieved by
  name/identifier; disclosure without written consent prohibited
  except twelve statutory exceptions (intra-agency need-to-know,
  court order, law-enforcement request, valid FOIA request, etc.).

## FOIA (5 U.S.C. § 552)
- Fixed the APA's toothless disclosure scheme; any person may
  request — no interest requirement. Agencies must release records
  they **possess and control** (*Tax Analysts*) unless an exemption
  applies; the withholding burden is the Government's.
- "Agency": executive agencies (not Congress, courts, states, the
  President's immediate advisory staff, or private parties absent
  day-to-day supervision — *Burka*). Components aren't separate:
  Army/Navy/AF FOIA suits run against DoD.
- "Records": contractor-held data can qualify via constructive
  control or full Government funding (*Burka*; *Hercules* phone
  directory); records-management contractor holdings count (OPEN
  Government Act). NOT records: personal memory-aid files, tangible
  evidence, non-agency documents (grand-jury transcripts). FOIA
  never requires **creating** records (*FlightSafety*) — retention
  is the Federal Records Act's job.
- **The nine exemptions**, acquisition highlights:
  1. Classified (segregability applies; courts police via a
     *Vaughn* index).
  2. Internal personnel rules — narrowed by *Milner v. Navy* to
     genuine HR matters.
  3. Other withholding statutes — the acquisition star is **10
     U.S.C. § 2305(g)** (formerly; now § 3309(g)): contractor
     **proposals** in DoD's possession are categorically exempt.
  4. **Trade secrets & confidential commercial/financial
     information** obtained from a person. Trade secret = narrow
     D.C. Circuit definition (tied to the making of trade
     commodities, *Public Citizen*); line-item pricing and fuel-pump
     drawings qualified. "Confidential": **required** submissions
     (bids/proposals) get the *National Parks* two-part test
     (impair future collection / competitive harm); **voluntary**
     submissions get the more protective *Critical Mass* test
     (would the submitter customarily release it?).
  5. Inter/intra-agency memos privileged in civil discovery —
     deliberative process, attorney-client, work product; extends
     to agency consultants (*Klamath* corollary).
  6. Personal privacy (clearly-unwarranted standard; protected
     contractor employees' names in *Sheet Metal Workers*, *L.A.
     Times* Iraq contractors).
  7. Law-enforcement records (7(A) pending proceedings, 7(C)
     stronger privacy — corporations have none, *FCC v. AT&T*;
     Glomar allowed; *Raytheon* — audit documents recompiled for
     an investigation qualify).
  8. Financial-institution regulation. 9. Well data.

## Gotchas / lessons learned
- A competitor's FOIA request for proposal material usually dies at
  Exemption 3 (§ 3309(g)) before Exemption 4 analysis is even
  needed — but only for proposals *in the agency's possession*;
  incorporated-into-contract material is different.
- Whether business information was submitted **voluntarily or by
  requirement** flips the confidentiality test (*Critical Mass* vs.
  *National Parks*) — determine which before advising on release.
- Deliberative-process protection ends at the decision: the CO's
  final decision and after-the-fact reports are fair game.
- FOIA requesters need no reason; never assume "they'd only want
  this for litigation" changes the analysis. Conversely, submitters
  should mark and legend everything — agencies notify submitters,
  but legends drive the Exemption 4 review.
- Remember the wiki's own hygiene rule: bid/proposal information and
  source selection information don't belong in these notes at all.
